## Deviations, additions and choices (none change a rule of the machine or the Balance, a prop location, or who lives and dies)
1. **Which line SESHAT lip-reads: Tut mouths "It will fail."** The bible leaves the mouthed line open. I chose it because it drives the next beat causally.
   - After the tell, Tut realises the forecast has no ages of its own. He mouths the conclusion to Nour, and SESHAT reads it.
   - SESHAT learns only that the heroes doubt its heart, not why. It never learns the rule of ages (bible §4).
   - Its uncertainty makes it secure its fallback heart at once ("Then I will keep a second heart close"). That is the capture attempt, and it gives Nour the leverage for her trade.
   - It also sets up SEQ 10's interrogation ("What did the block say?") and the move from 98% to "Certainty: one point zero" after her edited truth.
   - How SESHAT learned: at Karnak (SEQ 7) Tut mouthed the Hymn "half a beat ahead" of SESHAT's broadcast, giving it a known text aligned with his mouth, like a Rosetta Stone. SESHAT also corrects its SEQ 3 translation: "on the third of November, he did not say he was tired."
2. **"It read me and gave you the queen" is dramatised.** The forecast kneels to Tut and says the queen's own line from the memory minutes earlier, "You will be the one who remembers." Tut: "He never knelt. She did." The memory pays off in the present, and the audience gets the line without exposition.
3. **The Dendera-era word is the zodiacal Scales (Libra).** The bible asks for a Ptolemaic term chosen with the consultant.
   - Research 11 supports this concretely: the zodiac reached Egypt only in Ptolemaic times (Clagett), and Libra is carved as a balance on the Dendera zodiac (Louvre D 38; Priskin 2015). Demotic horoscopes even call the sign "horizon" (ȝḫ.t), which the film could echo.
   - It is thematically exact: the forecast talks about the sky "weighing".
   - Nour says "It's on a ceiling at Dendera", not "first attested at Dendera", because Clagett gives the rectangular Esna zodiac as the earliest. The Egyptian word itself is left to the consultant (`[[verify]]`).
4. **"The first I know from it. The second I know."** This line is added after Tut's verbatim bones line. It marks the modern KV55 dispute as learned and his father's reign as lived (bible §6 rule).
5. **SESHAT's rebuttal cites "JAMA, 2010: thirty-five to forty-five"** rather than the lead author, so no real former official is named.
6. **Nour is not targeted by the red lines** during the capture. SESHAT values her ("Now you are the only copy", SEQ 7). She sees this and uses it.
7. **The trade's terms:** "Let him go… All of them." SESHAT: "They may leave Amarna. Until the sun is up." This explains why the party walks away and why they must hide from the flies by day (bible 9.9). SESHAT's parting line, "You were sent to it" (echoing SEQ 8), is its reason for releasing the witness: it expects him at Giza anyway.
8. **The whisper in the dark:** Nour pulls Tut's hood up and puts her own head inside it, with her palm over his chest glow and the drone's rotors covering the sound. It is literally a whisper with no light. It pays off "Hood up: a shadow" (SEQ 4) and launches the "secrets only in darkness" rule.
9. **The Reis at Amarna.** The recurring hunter escorts the forecast and makes the capture attempt. Tarek's one round cracks its shoulder shell, which fits "damaged in each encounter" and SEQ 11's "cracked bone-white shell". It already has the stump at the wrist from SEQ 7. It boards the drone north (SEQ 10 has it at Saqqara the next night).
10. **Tomas glimpsed.** He is silent in the drone's door, and he and Nour draw the forecast aboard. This plants **the forecast's fear of the dark** ("It is dark in there"), which SEQ 11 pays off. Tomas is captive per §12. It is consistent with SEQ 8's "The father will need his physician".
11. **The memory's Gantenbrink door:** in 1332 BC a long bronze arm of the disk withdraws from a hand-wide shaft and the slab above slides. The machine's own limb bridged the door, rhyming with SEQ 11's inch-worm. I do **not** show the pins, the red marks or "Bridge the two" (SEQ 11 owns them). The Hall is shown only as niches in two rows and a black scale, with no count of 42, no glass slab and no Ammit, all left for SEQ 12.
12. **The mother:** the left side of her face is always turned from the torch, a silent nod to the KV35 Younger Lady's facial wound [01 §8] that is never shown. Her heart follows the §3.4 grammar exactly (hands under linen, light through fabric, the vessel).
13. **The queen's Year 16 name at the quarry.**
    - Tut leads them to "quarries in the hills" north of the city. At dawn, raking light shows a red-ochre builder's inscription naming the Great Royal Wife Neferneferuaten Nefertiti.
    - This is real: the Dayr Abu Hinnis graffito, found 2012 [02 §1, §7].
    - "She was still his wife. The next year she was king" follows the film's §2 fiction (Nefertiti reigning as Neferneferuaten).
    - The heading stays generic ("ABANDONED LIMESTONE QUARRY") to match SEQ 10.
14. **Plan A's target:** Adaeze deduces Saqqara from Tut's silence at the Serapeum screen in SEQ 2 ("You only go quiet at the true ones"; ledger rule §9). Tut stays quiet. "And sunrise?" / "One problem at a time." sets up SEQ 10's reversal without pre-empting it.
15. **Small prop and wardrobe business:**
    - Fathi's red scarf binds Tut's chest glow from the yard to the derailment, then goes back to his throat.
    - Adaeze uses **half** the Egyptian-blue jar. SEQ 10 then throws "the last jar".
    - Tarek's police handset carries SESHAT (SEQ 8 established it).
16. **Added one old third-class carriage,** so the bible's "uncouples the car" has a car. Tarek's line "My father drove these, Aswan to Cairo" is invented backstory. **Other writers: flag if Tarek's family is established differently.**
17. **Tarek's humour:** "It is now." is written as dry resolve, not a joke. SEQ 5, 7 and 8 already spent his Act II joke.
18. **Pawah is named but not quoted.** I cut the TT139 "lord of Persea trees" line for length. It remains available to SEQ 12 or the coda.
19. **Language:**
    - Tut–Nour and Tut–father exchanges are in Late Egyptian; the forecast speaks liturgical Middle Egyptian (its first tell); all subtitled.
    - Tarek and Fathi use Egyptian Arabic when talking to each other in the cab; group orders are in English.
    - "Here am I" is not spoken in this sequence, deliberately: the Deir Mawas shabti are silent, which reads as more ominous than a chorus, and it keeps SEQ 10's wave fresh.
