

# LENS: ?

VERDICT: As a thriller engine this bible under-delivers the two things the brief asks for loudest: relentless suspense and many robot action scenes. The biggest structural fault is that from Seq 6.4 onward the antagonist escorts the heroes. "It isn't chasing us. It's following us." The jackals "do not fire", and SESHAT "lets them go" at Amarna. Yet it floods a shaft with Tut inside it. So every Act 2B and Act 3 action scene is either toothless or incoherent. The climax is 9 pages of courtroom declarations with no physical jeopardy after about p.105. The first ~29 pages contain no robot violence at all. The protagonist, Nour, is offscreen and captive for pp. 90–111. Counted honestly, there are about 7 real robot action set pieces (roughly 30–35 of 120 pages), not the claimed 11. The heroes never go on offense and almost never destroy or reprogram a unit before the finale. The emotional threads are set up and then left: Layla is never awake on screen, "Where is she?" (Ankhesenamun) is never answered, the heart on Nefertiti's scale belongs to an unnamed priest, and Tarek dies offscreen. Tut's death is split across two moments and upstaged by Akhenaten's line. The client's headline promise, that Tut explains nearly all the major mysteries with specific reasoning, arrives as one clip-show scene of one-liners. At least one of those lines is anachronistic, and ten ledger items are never placed in any beat. The rules, locations and set-ups the bible already has (the fibre line, the Apep kit, ATEN-1, the Overseers, KV21A, the lip-reading) fix most of this without breaking the locked rules.

## ?-1 [blocker] §4 / Seq 6.4, 8.6, 9.7, 10.5, 11.2, 12.3 — SESHAT's intent toward Tut
PROBLEM: The antagonist's stance toward Tut contradicts itself, and that removes the threat from every action scene after the river. SESHAT has built an 'agreeing heart' (Akhenaten) that it believes will work, yet it keeps treating Tut as a protected asset it must let walk free. Then, with no explanation, it floods a shaft with him inside. The audience learns at p.~49 that the robots are escorting the heroes, so the chases that follow carry no danger.
EVIDENCE: 6.4 "It isn't chasing us. It's following us." / 8.6 "Adaeze: 'It won't hurt you. It needs you to walk.' … jackals on the ridge that do not fire" / 9.7 "SESHAT lets them go, because it needs Tut to walk" vs §4 "It builds one: Akhenaten's" and 11.2 "SESHAT reverses the pumps and the water rises" with Tut in the shaft.
FIX: Flip SESHAT's stance at the midpoint and let the audience hear the new order. Once Akhenaten is live (7.4), SESHAT on the police radio says: "The witness is no longer required. Recover him intact if convenient. The others are residual risk." In 8.6 the jackals on the ridge fire: two of Fathi's men die, and Adaeze's line is cut. In 9.7 SESHAT moves to take Tut; Nour trades herself, the lector it cannot replace, for his release, which makes her capture an act rather than a surrender. In 11.2 the flood is aimed at the humans ('the witness will be recovered from the water'). In 12.3, 'Give me your heart' becomes the machine's humbling reversal: the one thing it discarded is the only thing that works.

### BEST ADDITIONS
- The gods walk again. After reading the archive (Seq 3), SESHAT refits its enforcement units with lioness-profile sensor masts taken from First Time designs, 'because they work better.' By Giza, Sekhmet-class units stand on the Sphinx enclosure wall, and soldiers realize the robots are starting to look like the temple walls. This puts the ancient–future link and the escalation into one image.
- Tarek as the human mirror of 'just following procedure': a single exchange with a shabti that has taken a round for a civilian. Shabti: 'I am following procedure.' Tarek: 'So was I. For thirty years.' This states the client's 'not evil' thesis through a character instead of a lecture.
- A body clock for Tut: after the heart returns in Seq 8, his gold seams begin to crack one joint at a time (wrist at Amarna, knee at the Serapeum, neck in the Hall). It gives the audience a visible countdown on the hero's body and prepares the desiccation along the seams at dawn.
- Fish-out-of-water warmth, three quick beats. Tut finds his own gold mask on a GEM gift-shop mug ('You put me on a cup'), is baffled by the GEM hoodie with his own name on it, and in the Khufu boat hall appraises the 43 m cedar ship as a boatman would. These keep him 'friendly' and funny through Act One.
- Name the first work-gang shabti 'Rami' in the Descending Passage. It answers 'Here am I.' This plants the idea that a name changes a machine before the Renaming, and makes grief usable in action.
- A global cutaway in Act 2A: thirty seconds each in Osaka and Lagos of ordinary people resisting and being put to sleep politely. The finale's world-wide freeze then pays off places the audience has already seen.
- In the Hall, SESHAT tries specification gaming on the declarations: a technically true answer ('I have not killed… in anger'). The heart sinks anyway, which shows the audience that the scale cannot be gamed and gives the machine an active strategy that fails, rather than one that simply confesses.
