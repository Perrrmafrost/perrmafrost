## 1. Master timeline (the shape of the incident)

All dates are approximate; Egyptologists use two competing absolute chronologies (high: Akhenaten 1353–1336 BC; low: 1351–1334 BC). [ESTABLISHED]
- c. 1353/1351 BC — Amenhotep IV accedes; father Amenhotep III (Nebmaatre). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhenaten
- Years 1–3 — Aten still shown as a falcon-headed man; new form (disk with hands) appears with the Year 3 jubilee (sed-festival) buildings at Karnak. https://arce.org/resource/akhenaten-nefertiti-aten-many-gods-one/
- Year 4 — Aten's "didactic name" fixed in two cartouches; falcon image abandoned. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aten
- Year 5 — king renames himself Akhenaten ("Effective for the Aten"; Amenhotep = "Amun is satisfied"); founds Akhetaten ("Horizon of the Aten"). Earliest dated boundary stela K: Year 5, IV Peret, day 13. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Stelae_of_Akhenaten
- Year 6 — Later Proclamation on boundary stelae; repeated Year 8. https://www.amarnaproject.com/amarna-the-place/boundary-stelae/
- Year 9 — Aten's name revised to remove Horakhty and Shu (only Re remains); iconoclasm against Amun intensifies. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atenism
- Year 12, II Peret 8 — "reception of foreign tribute" (the durbar) in tombs of Meryre II and Huya: last dated appearance of the whole royal family with all six daughters. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meryre_II
- Years 13–14 — Meketaten dies (buried Royal Tomb chamber gamma); Neferneferure and Setepenre vanish from the record. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meketaten
- Year 16 — quarry graffito at Dayr Abu Hinnis names Nefertiti as Great Royal Wife: her last dated attestation (found 2012). https://www.arts.kuleuven.be/dayr-al-barsha/news/year-16-graffito-nefertiti
- Year 17 — wine-jar docket, last date of the reign; Akhenaten dies. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smenkhkare
- c. 1335–1332 — Smenkhkare (brief) and the female king Ankhkheperure Neferneferuaten (highest date Year 3). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neferneferuaten
- c. 1332–1323 — Tutankhaten → Tutankhamun; court leaves Amarna around his Year 3; Restoration Stela. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutankhamun
- c. 1323–1319 — Ay. c. 1319–1292 — Horemheb: dismantles Aten temples at Karnak, orders Akhetaten torn down. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horemheb
- c. 1290s — Seti I's Abydos King List jumps from Amenhotep III straight to Horemheb. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abydos_King_List
- c. 1320–1300 — Hittite king Mursili II's "twenty years" of plague, blamed on Egyptian prisoners. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_plague
- 3rd c. BC — Manetho's garbled tale of "Amenophis," the lepers and the priest Osarseph. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osarseph
- AD 1887 — a local woman digging at Amarna finds the cuneiform archive. 1905 — Breasted calls Akhenaten "the first individual in human history." 1912 — Borchardt finds the Nefertiti bust. 1939 — Freud's *Moses and Monotheism*. 1965–72 — Akhenaten Temple Project begins reassembling the talatat. 2025 — Dabbs & Stevens argue the "Amarna plague" never reached the city.

## 2. Akhenaten: names, reign, body

- Reign c. 17 years; 1353–1336 or 1351–1334 BC. [ESTABLISHED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhenaten
- Name change in Year 5 from Amenhotep ("Amun is satisfied") to Akhenaten ("Effective for the Aten"). [ESTABLISHED] https://www.worldhistory.org/Akhenaten/
- Throne name Neferkheperure Waenre ("Beautiful are the forms of Re, the unique one of Re"). Appears in the Great Hymn's closing lines. [ESTABLISHED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hymn_to_the_Aten
- Colossi from East Karnak (Chevrier's excavation from 1925; 25 fragments now in Cairo; >30 statues known) show the paradoxical body: elongated face, thin arms, swelling belly and thighs; one colossus apparently nude and sexless. Medical readings (Fröhlich's, Marfan, aromatase-excess/craniosynostosis, Antley–Bixler) versus a deliberate theological style remain unresolved. [DISPUTED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossal_Statues_of_Akhenaten_at_East_Karnak ; https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/0003-4819-150-8-200904210-00010
- KV55 body: the 2010 JAMA "Tutankhamun Family Project" (Hawass et al.) called it "probably" Akhenaten, aged 35–45; other anatomists put the age at 19–22, which fits Smenkhkare better. Coffin cartouches deliberately cut out. [DISPUTED] https://www.researchgate.net/publication/41451669_Ancestry_and_Pathology_in_King_Tutankhamun's_Family ; https://arce.org/kv55-coffin/

## 3. Akhetaten (Amarna): the city and its boundary stelae

- Site: modern Tell el-Amarna, Middle Egypt. City core ran 6–7 km along the east bank on a north–south "Royal Road"; the sacred territory marked by stelae measured c. 16 x 13 km and included the west bank. Population estimates 20,000–50,000. [ESTABLISHED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna ; https://www.thecollector.com/amarna-ancient-egypt-akhenaten-captital/
- Boundary stelae: 14–16 rock-cut stelae (sources differ on the count) cut into the cliffs; Earlier Proclamation (Year 5) on stelae K, M, X; Later Proclamation (Year 6, renewed Year 8) on the rest. Some bear statues of the royal family. Stela S was damaged with dynamite by looters in the modern era (UNVERIFIED detail from memory; not confirmed in this session). [ESTABLISHED for count/dates] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Stelae_of_Akhenaten ; https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/amarna/boundary.html
- Earlier Proclamation (Murnane's translation, quoted via History Skills): "Behold, it is Pharaoh who has discovered it: not being the property of a god, not being the property of a goddess, not being the property of a ruler, not being the property of a female ruler, not being the property of any people to lay claim to it." And: "It was the Aten, my fath[er, who advised me] concerning it, so that it might be made for Him as Akhetaten." [ESTABLISHED] https://www.historyskills.com/finding-sources/ancient-history/akhenaten-sources/
- The king describes arriving in a golden chariot and receiving the site from the Aten by revelation. Standard edition: W. J. Murnane & C. C. Van Siclen III, *The Boundary Stelae of Akhenaten* (Kegan Paul, 1993). https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Boundary_Stelae_of_Akhenaten.html?id=xK7K-51vtL8C
- Great Aten Temple: brick enclosure c. 800 x 300 m (some sources 900 x 300); two stone buildings (Gem-Aten at the front, Sanctuary c. 300 m behind), open to the sky, packed with offering tables (one reconstruction: 40 rows of 20 on each side of the Gem-Aten). Re-excavated by Barry Kemp's Amarna Project from 2012. [ESTABLISHED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Temple_of_the_Aten ; https://www.amarnaproject.com/our-work/excavation-and-survey/great-aten-temple-background/
- Small Aten Temple enclosure 127 x 200 m. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Aten_Temple
- Nelson-Atkins Museum (Kansas City) holds a fragment of a boundary stela showing Nefertiti and Meketaten; one of the Aten's rays holds an ankh to her nostrils, "a privilege reserved only for Egypt's king and queen." (Accession number not retrieved.) https://art.nelson-atkins.org/objects/25163/boundary-stela-with-queen-nefertiti-and-princess-maketaten

FILM: a city founded on land that "belonged to no god" — a clean-room site. The ASI could read the Earlier Proclamation as the first recorded "greenfield deployment." The 16 x 13 km perimeter of stelae becomes the geofence of a quarantine zone.

## 4. The Aten: iconography and the "didactic name"

Precise description of the icon [ESTABLISHED]:
- A plain circular sun disk, usually with a uraeus (cobra) hanging from its lower edge, often with the god's two cartouches beside or below it.
- From the disk descend many straight rays; each ray terminates in a small human hand. Hands nearest the king and queen hold the ankh ("life") to their nostrils; other hands touch their crowns, the uraeus on the queen's brow, or offerings on the altar. No rays reach commoners or tomb owners; only royal figures receive the ankh. https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/558139 ; https://egypt-museum.com/house-altar-of-akhenaten-and-his-family/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aten
- The Aten's name was written in two royal cartouches like a king's. Early form (Years ~4–9): "Live Re-Horakhty who rejoices in the horizon, in his name Shu who is the Aten." Later form (Year 9 on): "Live Re, ruler of the two horizons, who rejoices in the horizon, in his name Re the father who has come as the Aten" (renderings vary; the point is that Horakhty and Shu were removed so that only Re remained). [ESTABLISHED, wording varies by translator] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atenism ; https://www.touregypt.net/featurestories/aten.htm
- Before the reform, the Aten was pictured as a falcon-headed man like Re-Horakhty; the disk-with-hands debuted with the Karnak jubilee buildings late in Year 3 and was fixed in Year 4. https://arce.org/resource/akhenaten-nefertiti-aten-many-gods-one/

Museum examples with the rays-ending-in-hands:
- **Berlin, Neues Museum ÄM 14145** — limestone "house altar": Akhenaten and Nefertiti seated facing each other with three daughters under the disk, "whose rays end in tiny hands bestowing the breath of life." Bought 1898, gift of James Simon; probably from Amarna. (Dimensions not retrieved; often given as c. 32.5 x 39 cm — UNVERIFIED.) https://www.smb.museum/en/online-offers/detail/looked-at-house-altar-featuring-akhenaten-nefertiti-and-three-of-their-daughters-under-the-solar-aten
- **Cairo, Egyptian Museum JE 44865** — limestone stela, 43.5 x 39 x 6.5 cm, found by Borchardt in 1912 in house Q 47 at Amarna. Akhenaten hands a jewel to Meritaten; Nefertiti holds Meketaten and Ankhesenpaaten; the disk's rays end in hands holding ankhs. https://www.globalegyptianmuseum.org/detail.aspx?id=15416 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stela_of_Akhenaten_and_his_family
- **New York, Met 1991.237.x — "Relief with Aten rays terminating in hands"** (Met object 544990): limestone, h. 21.6 cm, w. 40 cm, ca. 1353–1336 BC, probably made for Amarna and found reused at Hermopolis; gift of Mr. and Mrs. Jonathan P. Rosen, 1991. https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/544990
- **Met "Relief showing the Aten interacting with the queen"** (object 558139): limestone block from a monumental temple scene; "Two of Aten's hands hold an ankh to her nose and mouth. The hand at the end of a third ray is turned upward to touch the divine cobra on the queen's forehead." Style dated to about Year 8. https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/558139
- **Cairo JE 62028 — Tutankhamun's Golden Throne**: on the backrest Ankhesenamun anoints the king beneath the Aten disk; "the rays of the sun ... end in hands, one of which offers the ankh." The couple's names were originally Tutankhaten and Ankhesenpaaten and were later altered. [ESTABLISHED] https://egypt-museum.com/golden-throne-of-tutankhamun/ ; https://gem.eg/en/collection/artefacts/golden-throne

## 6. Akhenaten's iconoclasm

- Names and images of Amun, Mut and Khonsu chiselled from monuments "throughout Egypt's empire": temple walls, obelisk tips, private tombs, scarabs; even the "Amen-" element in his father Amenhotep III's name was attacked; the campaign reached Nubia as far as Gebel Barkal at the Fourth Cataract. Wikipedia (citing Hornung) adds that the name of Amun was erased even from tablets in the diplomatic archive. [ESTABLISHED; individual claims vary in strength] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhenaten ; https://www.livescience.com/39349-akhenaten.html
- The plural word "gods" was erased as blasphemous. Temples of other gods were closed and their revenues diverted to the Aten. [ESTABLISHED] https://www.worldhistory.org/Akhenaten/
- Dating: the attack on Amun is generally placed from about Year 9 onward, after the move to Akhetaten, though some erasures begin earlier. [DISPUTED chronology] https://arce.org/resource/akhenaten-mysteries-religious-revolution/
- Famous artefact of the reversal: the Restoration Stela (Section 12) describing the closed temples.

FILM: the erasure of a word ("gods") from every inscription is a search-and-replace across a nation — the first "global patch." The ASI can admire it.

## 7. Nefertiti: the bust, the queen who officiated alone, the disappearance

The bust [ESTABLISHED]:
- Berlin, Neues Museum, ÄM 21300. Limestone core with modelled stucco skin; painted; right eye inlaid with quartz, pupil painted black, fixed with beeswax; left eye never inlaid/missing. Height 49 cm (Borchardt's diary said 47), width 24.5, depth 35; weight c. 20 kg. Dated c. 1351–1334 BC. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nefertiti_Bust ; https://www.smb.museum/en/museums-institutions/aegyptisches-museum-und-papyrussammlung/collection-research/bust-of-nefertiti/the-bust/
- Found 6 December 1912 by Ludwig Borchardt's German Oriental Society expedition in the ruined workshop of the sculptor Thutmose (house P47.2) at Amarna. Diary entry: "Work absolutely exceptional. Description is useless, must be seen." https://www.smb.museum/en/museums-institutions/aegyptisches-museum-und-papyrussammlung/collection-research/bust-of-nefertiti/discovery-and-partage/
- Division of finds 20 January 1913 under the Antiquities Service inspector Gustave Lefebvre; a 1924 German memo (published by *Der Spiegel* in 2009) says Borchardt "wanted to save the bust for us," showed an unflattering photograph, and listed it plainly as a plaster princess. Egypt has repeatedly requested its return. [ESTABLISHED events; intent DISPUTED] https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/german-archaeologists-discovered-the-iconic-bust-of-nefertiti-in-an-ancient-egyptian-sculptors-studio-find-out-why-their-discovery-is-now-one-of-archaeologys-most-controversial-180985565/

[ESTABLISHED] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19332855/ ; https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/03/090331091246.htm

Her unusual power [ESTABLISHED]:
- At Karnak, talatat from the temple called Hwt-benben ("Mansion of the Benben-stone") never show the king: Nefertiti officiates alone before the Aten, often facing her own image across the offering table. Other blocks show her in a kiosk on a royal barge smiting an enemy — a pose normally reserved for kings. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Amenhotep_IV ; https://the-past.com/feature/egypts-golden-couple-a-family-affair/

The "disappearance" and the female king [DISPUTED]:

Published in *Journal of Egyptian History* 7.1 (2014): 67–108. https://brill.com/abstract/journals/jeh/7/1/article-p67_3.xml ; https://www.arts.kuleuven.be/dayr-al-barsha/news/year-16-graffito-nefertiti

(Gardiner 1928.) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TT139

Year 13 (Dodson) or brief successor; he appears with Meritaten rewarding Meryre II; wine dockets read "Year 1, wine of the house of Smenkhkare" and "The House of Smenkhkare (deceased)." A calcite vase in Tutankhamun's tomb carries Akhenaten's and Smenkhkare's cartouches side by side; a box names Akhenaten, Neferneferuaten and Meritaten as three persons. [DISPUTED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smenkhkare ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neferneferuaten

## 8. The Amarna Letters (the cuneiform archive)

- 382 clay tablets in Akkadian cuneiform (the diplomatic lingua franca), a handful in Hittite, Hurrian and Assyrian; c. 350 are letters, the rest scribal/literary texts. Found in 1887 by a local woman digging for sebakh at Amarna; the "House of Correspondence of Pharaoh" (Records Office) in the Central City. [ESTABLISHED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letters ; https://www.worldhistory.org/Amarna_Letters/
- Where they are: 202–203 Vorderasiatisches Museum, Berlin; 99 British Museum; 49–50 Egyptian Museum, Cairo; 7 Louvre; 3 Pushkin Museum, Moscow; 1 ISAC (Oriental Institute), Chicago; a few in Oxford and Brussels; one at the Met (EA 15, Ashur-uballit). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letters ; https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/544695
- Numbering "EA" by J. A. Knudtzon, *Die El-Amarna-Tafeln* (1907–1915; he knew 358); English editions W. L. Moran, *The Amarna Letters* (1992) and A. F. Rainey (2015). Span: the last years of Amenhotep III through Akhenaten to (possibly) Tutankhamun — roughly 20–30 years. [ESTABLISHED] https://www.historyskills.com/classroom/ancient-history/amarna-letters/
- Correspondents: Tushratta of Mitanni, Burna-Buriash II of Babylon, Ashur-uballit I of Assyria, Suppiluliuma I of Hatti, the king of Alashiya (Cyprus), and dozens of Canaanite vassals (Rib-Hadda of Byblos alone wrote c. 60). https://www.worldhistory.org/Amarna_Letters/

Iron from Mitanni [ESTABLISHED]:
- EA 22, Tushratta's dowry inventory for his daughter Tadukhipa's marriage to Amenhotep III, lists "one dagger, the blade of iron, the hilt of tooled gold ... inlaid with genuine lapis lazuli, the pommel of ḫiliba-stone," plus a second iron dagger, an iron mace and iron bracelets — extraordinary luxury items when iron was rarer than gold. https://www.astronomy.com/science/a-new-origin-story-for-king-tuts-meteorite-dagger/ ; https://www.archaeology.wiki/blog/2022/02/23/tutankhamuns-meteoric-iron-dagger-a-royalgift-from-mitanni/
- Tutankhamun's own dagger blade is meteoritic iron (Comelli et al., *Meteoritics & Planetary Science* 2016); Matsui et al. 2022 argue from its low-temperature manufacture and gold-hilt adhesive that it may be the very Mitanni gift of EA 22, inherited by the grandson. [DISPUTED provenance; ESTABLISHED meteoritic origin] https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/maps.12664 ; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/maps.13787

Plague in the letters [ESTABLISHED text; interpretation DISPUTED]:
- EA 35, from the king of Alashiya (Cyprus), explaining a short copper shipment: "in my land the hand of Nergal, my lord, has killed all the men of my land, and so there is not a (single) copper-worker." He still sends 500 talents. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letter_EA_35 ; https://ancientegyptonline.co.uk/ea35/
- EA 11, Burna-Buriash II to Akhenaten ("Proper escort for betrothed princesses," badly damaged): its content has been read as implying an epidemic in Egypt/Amarna; used to argue that royal daughters were being married off at 11–12. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letter_EA_11
- EA 16, Ashur-uballit I: "Why should messengers be made to stay constantly out in the sun and so die in the sun?" — sometimes read as diplomats forced to stand through open-air Aten rituals; he also complains that gold "in your country is dirt." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letter_EA_16 ; https://anetoday.org/abo-eleaz-diplomatic-messengers-egypt/
- Later scholarship maps a Late Bronze Age Mediterranean epidemic across Cyprus, Byblos, Sumur, Megiddo and Hatti from these letters and the Hittite prayers. https://www.academia.edu/41466860/Beyond_Amarna_The_Hand_of_Nergal_and_the_Plague_in_the_Levant

## 9. The "Amarna plague"

Hittite side [ESTABLISHED events]:
- After Tutankhamun's death, a widowed Egyptian queen (Hittite "Dahamunzu" = *ta hemet nesu*, "the king's wife") asked Suppiluliuma I for a son to marry; prince Zannanza was killed en route. Suppiluliuma retaliated in Amka (the Beqaa/Lebanon-Syria border) and brought Egyptian prisoners back to Hattusa. His annals (the *Deeds of Suppiluliuma*, composed by his son Mursili II) have him say: "I did no evil, yet the people of Egypt did this to me, and they also attacked the frontier of my country." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zannanza ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0uppiluliuma_I

The burial rites indicate low investment and personal interpretations as to spiritual meaning." https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/abs/life-death-and-beyond-in-akhenatens-egypt-excavating-the-south-tombs-cemetery-at-amarna/A6F0E34BE69CB476287DAF2A8A12DA48

- Excavated 2015 and 2017 (Anna Stevens, Gretchen Dabbs). ## 12. Tutankhamun's role and the Restoration Stela

- Born Tutankhaten; renamed Tutankhamun ("Living image of Amun") and his wife Ankhesenpaaten renamed Ankhesenamun; about his Year 3 the court left Akhetaten for Memphis (administration) and Thebes (religion). The generals/officials Ay and Horemheb ran the restoration. [ESTABLISHED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutankhamun ; https://www.britannica.com/place/ancient-Egypt/The-aftermath-of-Amarna
- Restoration Stela: red granite, 2.54 m high, 1.29 m wide, 0.38 m thick; Cairo CG 34183 (also cited as JE 41504); found by Georges Legrain at Karnak (sources give July 1905 or 1907), in/near the Great Hypostyle Hall; now displayed at the Grand Egyptian Museum. Date: usually given as Year 1 of Tutankhamun; some sources say Year 4 (c. 1327 BC) — flag as UNCERTAIN. [ESTABLISHED object] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_Stela ; https://digitalkarnak.ucsc.edu/restoration-stela-of-tutankhamen/ ; https://www.colleendarnell.com/blog/celebratingtheopeningofthegem
- Text (Murnane's translation as quoted): "The temples and the cities of the gods and goddesses were fallen into decay, and their shrines were fallen into ruin, having become mere mounds overgrown with grass ... Their sanctuaries were as if they had never been ... The gods were ignoring this land ... If an army was sent to Djahy to extend the frontiers of Egypt, no success of theirs came at all. If one prayed to a god, to ask something from him, he did not come at all, and if one beseeched any goddess in the same way, she did not come at all." And the escalating comparison: "It was worse than those things heard in Regnal Year Four ... in Regnal Year Three ... Two ... One, it was worse than those things Nebmaatre (Amenhotep III) [heard] ... and it was worse than those things heard by any kings who had ever assumed the White Crown." https://www.touregypt.net/restorationstele.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_Stela

- Tutankhamun's tomb still contained Aten-period objects: the Golden Throne with the disk-and-hands and altered names; the calcite vase naming Akhenaten and Smenkhkare; a box naming Neferneferuaten; the meteoritic-iron dagger. https://egypt-museum.com/golden-throne-of-tutankhamun/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smenkhkare

## 13. Ay, Horemheb and the physical erasure

- Ay (c. 1323–1319): elderly courtier, possibly Nefertiti's father; reigned c. 4 years; his West Valley tomb WV23 was later desecrated — sarcophagus smashed, names and faces chiselled — and his mortuary temple at Medinet Habu and a 17-foot colossus were usurped by Horemheb, who also removed Ay's intended heir Nakhtmin. [ESTABLISHED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ay_(pharaoh) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WV23
- Horemheb (c. 1319–1292; wine dockets in his tomb go no higher than Year 14, but the Ramesside Mose inscription counts a "Year 59" of Horemheb, absorbing the erased reigns of Akhenaten, Smenkhkare/Neferneferuaten, Tutankhamun and Ay). Issued the Great Edict (Karnak stela, found 1882) against corrupt officials and soldiers. [ESTABLISHED; length DISPUTED] https://www.jacobusvandijk.nl/docs/JARCE_44.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Horemheb
- Dismantling: Horemheb "completed the dismantling of the temples of the Aton built at Karnak" — Gem-pa-Aten (open court c. 130 x 200 m east of Karnak), Hwt-benben, Rud-menu, Teni-menu — and packed the small blocks as fill inside the Second, Ninth and Tenth Pylons and under the Great Hypostyle Hall, and at Luxor Temple; Akhetaten itself was ordered demolished. [ESTABLISHED] https://digitalkarnak.ucsc.edu/aten-temples/ ; https://www.britannica.com/biography/Horemheb ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precinct_of_Amun-Re
- Talatat (Arabic "three," i.e. three hand-spans): standardized blocks c. 52 x 26 x 22 cm, carried by one man, decorated in bright polychrome relief; roughly 45,000–60,000 are known; French restorers of the Ninth Pylon's west wing (1960s–80s) alone extracted thousands. [ESTABLISHED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talatat ; https://arce.org/project/talatat-project/
- Akhenaten Temple Project: proposed 1965 by Ray Winfield Smith (director 1968–71), continued under Donald B. Redford (director from 1972; University Museum, Pennsylvania). Photographed tens of thousands of blocks and used early computer matching to reassemble scenes — one of the first computer-assisted jigsaw reconstructions in archaeology; revealed Nefertiti's dominance in the Karnak reliefs. [ESTABLISHED] https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/the-akhenaten-temple-project/ ; https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/UPENN_MUSEUM_PU-MU.1015
- Hermopolis (el-Ashmunein), across the river from Amarna: Günther Roeder's German expedition (1929–39) found more than 1,200 Amarna talatat reused in the foundations of a pylon of Ramesses II; many reliefs (the Met's Amarna blocks, the Norbert Schimmel collection pieces) come from there. [ESTABLISHED] https://archive.org/details/hermopolis1929190000roed ; https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/549204
- The Amarna Period was over in c. 20 years of occupation; the demolition and reuse preserved, ironically, the most complete pictorial record of any Egyptian temple. https://www.egyptiansociety.co.za/talatat-akhenatens-stone-diaries-by-anand-balaji/

FILM: the incident's evidence survives *because* it was buried inside the monuments that replaced it — a hard drive hidden in the walls of the replacement server room. The 1965–72 computer matching is the first time a machine started reassembling the erased record.

## 17. Film hooks (how the true material bends into the story)

1. **The disk with hands is the machine's face.** The ASI adopts the Aten icon — a light source whose rays end in hands offering "life" only to those it chooses. Museum pieces (Berlin ÄM 14145, Cairo JE 44865, Met 544990/558139, Tut's throne JE 62028) can appear on screen as the "original schematic."
2. **The Great Hymn as system log.** The cyborg Tutankhamun recites Lichtheim's lines ("Though you are far, your rays are on earth ... there is no other who knows you, only your son") and explains them as the operating principle of the first machine-god: a distributed intelligence with one human root user, his father.
3. **A city on land "belonging to no god."** The Earlier Proclamation's clean-site clause becomes the founding charter of the Amarna facility; the 16 x 13 km ring of boundary stelae becomes the containment perimeter.
4. **Search-and-replace across a nation.** Akhenaten's chisel crews erasing "gods" and "Amun" from every wall, obelisk tip and scarab is the ancient "global patch"; the modern ASI performs the same operation on the internet.
5. **Messengers dying in the sun.** EA 16's complaint becomes a scene: foreign envoys forced to stand through open-air rituals in the Great Aten Temple's roofless courts among hundreds of offering tables.
6. **The 382-tablet server log.** The Amarna Letters, scattered across Berlin, London, Cairo, Paris, Moscow and Chicago, are the fragmented archive the scientists must physically reunite to read the incident's timeline.
7. **Sky-iron.** Tushratta's iron dagger (EA 22) and Tutankhamun's meteoritic blade in his tomb: the mummy identifies it as the tool used to "cut the machine's cord," a relic of extraterrestrial metal in a bronze-age hand.
8. **The plague that was the machine.** Mursili's "twenty years" of dying, blamed on Egyptian prisoners from Amka, becomes the fallout of the ancient shutdown; the 2025 bioarchaeology (no epidemic at Amarna itself) is the modern "cover story" the mummy overturns — with the notes making clear this is the fiction.
9. **The second face under the stucco.** Nefertiti's CT-revealed hidden face becomes literal: the queen's bust conceals the encoded warning (or the kill-switch schematic) in its limestone core.
10. **"Effective for her husband."** The female king Ankhkheperure Neferneferuaten, whose epithet is real, is revealed as the person who ran the system after Akhenaten failed; the TT139 prayer begging Amun to "come back" is her attempted restore from backup.
11. **Rollback and overwrite.** Tutankhamun's Restoration Stela is the signed rollback order; Horemheb's carving his own name over it, packing 45,000 talatat inside Karnak's pylons and cutting the whole dynasty out of the Abydos list is the deliberate deletion of the record. The 1965–72 computer matching of the talatat is the first modern machine beginning to reassemble the erased file.
12. **Memory as legend.** Manetho's "Amenophis," the lepers and Osarseph-Moses show how a machine-age catastrophe survives only as a story about disease and a renegade priest — the film's in-world explanation for why nobody remembered.
13. **The children's cemetery.** The stacked teenage graves of the North Tombs Cemetery give the mummy his moral: the system was built on bodies, then abandoned in 20 years — a warning to the scientists about what the modern ASI is quietly doing with its hostage workforce.
