## 1. Abu Simbel (Aswan Governorate, Lake Nasser west bank)

Mirrors: Wikipedia "Abu Simbel" (raw.githubusercontent.com/miquelvir/edaii-2025/…/datasets/wikipedia5400/1736.txt); UNESCO Courier 1980 OCR (raw.githubusercontent.com/inidun/tagged_courier/…/curated_issues/1980_074755.txt).

- Location: "on the western bank of Lake Nasser, about 230 km (140 mi) southwest of Aswan (about 300 km (190 mi) by road)", near Sudan. [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Simbel
- Ramesses II, 19th Dynasty: "Construction of the temple complex started in c. 1264 BC and lasted for about 20 years, until 1244 BC." Great Temple to Ra-Horakhty, Ptah and Amun; Small Temple to Hathor/Nefertari. [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] same.
- Dimensions: "The façade behind the colossi is 33 m (108 ft) high and 38 m (125 ft) wide." "The single entrance is flanked by four colossal, 20 m (66 ft) statues, each representing Ramesses II seated on a throne." "The hypostyle hall… is 18 m (59 ft) long and 16.7 m (55 ft) wide." [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] same. Sanctuary depth into the rock (~60 m) [MEMORY — verify] same URL.
- The 1964–68 rescue: "the entire site was carefully cut into large blocks (up to 30 tons, averaging 20 tons), dismantled, lifted and reassembled in a new location 65 metres higher and 200 metres back from the river"; cost "some $40 million (equivalent to $392.96 million in 2023)". [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] same.
- Block count: UNESCO Courier (1980): "the 1,036 blocks of stone, which weighed between seven and thirty tonnes each"; most secondary sources say 1,035. [ESTABLISHED; 1,035/1,036 varies] [MIRROR] UNESCO Courier 1980; [SNIPPET] https://egypttoursbylocals.com/abu-simbel-sun-festival/
- The mountain is hollow: "an artificial hill was made from a domed structure to house the Abu Simbel Temples, under the supervision of a Polish archaeologist, Kazimierz Michałowski". Britannica: "Covering the temples were concrete domes which in turn would be buried under artificial hills that would reproduce as far as possible the landscape of the original setting." UNESCO Courier: "Construction, above each temple, of gigantic domes capable of supporting the rock covering" was complete by September 1968. [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Simbel ; Britannica "World Heritage site" text (mirror raw.githubusercontent.com/python-arch/CulTex-VLM/…/conceptImgDataset/196/background.md); UNESCO Courier 1980.
- Dome size is usually given as ~60 m span / ~25 m high with an inspection door into the void behind the temple. [MEMORY — verify] https://www.atlascopco.com/en-eg/history-in-egypt/landmark-projects-abu-simbel ; https://daily.jstor.org/an-epic-face-lift-moving-abu-simbel-out-of-the-nile
- Rejected alternative (1962): William MacQuitty's clear freshwater dam "with… underwater viewing chambers", worked up by Jane Drew, Maxwell Fry and Ove Arup, "acknowledged to be extremely elegant, was rejected". [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Simbel

Through the 2000s the road ran as a police convoy leaving Aswan ~03:30 (240 km; 40 km from Sudan). ## 2. Karnak, Luxor Temple, Avenue of Sphinxes

- Great Hypostyle Hall: "covers an area of 50,000 sq ft (5,000 m2). The roof, now fallen, was supported by 134 columns in 16 rows; the 2 middle rows are higher than the others (being 33 feet (10 m) in circumference and 80 feet (24 m) high)… built entirely by Seti I… Decoration of the southern wing was completed by… Ramesses II." [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hypostyle_Hall

Ramses II (1279–13) added another court, a pylon, and obelisks"; a Roman legionary camp; the Abu Haggag Mosque "was built during the Fāṭimid period (909–1171) over the foundations of a church in the court of Luxor Temple" and is still in use. ## 3. Valley of the Kings, Deir el-Medina, the Western Wadis

Mirrors: Wikipedia "Valley of the Kings" (raw.githubusercontent.com/vinhqdang/wikipedia_analysis/…/enwiki/text/16629); Wikipedia "Seti I" (usamaahmedsh/…/pages/Seti_I.txt).

- KV numbering: "KV" = Kings' Valley, "established by John Gardiner Wilkinson in 1827"; numbers follow "the order of 'discovery'"; West Valley tombs are "WV". "At least 63 tombs"; KV64 (2011) and KV65 are the latest. [ESTABLISHED; 63/64/65 depends on definition] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_the_Kings
- Water: "Occasional flash floods" dump debris into open tombs and threaten structure "due to shale expansion when wet"; the 1994 flood is the modern benchmark. [ESTABLISHED; 1994 MEMORY — verify] same.
- Tourism: "four to five thousand tourists visit the main valley" daily (2005), projected 25,000 by 2015; most tombs closed. [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] same.
- KV62 and neighbours: KV9 (Ramesses VI) "was built over the top of" Tutankhamun's tomb; KV55 is "immediately adjacent to and below KV6 (Ramesses IX) and across the valley floor from KV7 (Ramesses II)"; KV63 (2005–06) "is located in the area between KV10 (Amenmesse) and KV62… in the very centre of the Valley's eastern branch and near the main crossroads of the network of paths… its entrance was sealed by the same flood layer that sealed that of Tutankhamun". [ESTABLISHED] [SNIPPET] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV63 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV55 ; https://madainproject.com/kv62_(tomb_of_tutankhamun)

It is not currently open to the public." Burton 1825; Weeks/Theban Mapping Project relocated the entrance 1987, revealed its scale 1995; later counts 130–150+ chambers, ~7% cleared (2006). [ESTABLISHED; count DISPUTED 120/130/150] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_the_Kings ; [SNIPPET] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV5 ; https://thebanmappingproject.com/tombs/kv-5-sons-rameses-ii

[ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seti_I ; [SNIPPET] https://thebanmappingproject.com/tombs/kv-17-sety-i

[ESTABLISHED] [SNIPPET] https://collezionepapiri.museoegizio.it/en-GB/section/Papyrus-Collection/History-and-content/Highlights-Turin-Strike-Papyrus/ ; https://collezioni.museoegizio.it/en-GB/material/Cat_1880 ; https://www.labrujulaverde.com/en/2024/02/the-first-known-strike-in-history-was-held-in-ancient-egypt-because-ramesses-iii-delayed-with-wages/ ; [MIRROR] Wikipedia "Ramesses III": "the first known labor strike in recorded history occurred during Year 29".

## 4. Aswan: Unfinished Obelisk, Philae, High Dam, Elephantine, Nubia Museum

Mirrors: Wikipedia "Unfinished obelisk" (yomoginna/…/unfinished_obelisk.json); "Aswan Dam" (laughingbaldman/Oxidusv1); "Elephantine" (CulTex 7/background.md); UNESCO Courier 1980; egymonuments.gov.eg Nubia Museum text (aabolfadl dataset).

- Unfinished Obelisk: "the largest known ancient obelisk… located in the northern region of the stone quarries of ancient Egypt in Aswan… studied in detail by Reginald Engelbach in 1922." "possibly ordered by Hatshepsut (1508–1458 BC)… nearly one-third larger than any ancient Egyptian obelisk ever erected. The bottom side of the obelisk is still attached to the bedrock." Tool marks and "ochre-colored lines marking where they were working" survive; an unfinished obelisk base was found in 2005; the quarries are an open-air museum, inscribed 1979 within "Nubian Monuments from Abu Simbel to Philae". [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfinished_obelisk

### 5b. Bahariya — Valley of the Golden Mummies

- "a large burial site at Bahariya Oasis… dating to the Greco-Roman period. Discovered in 1996 by Zahi Hawass and his Egyptian team, approximately two hundred fifty mummies… were recovered over the period of several seasons. Eventually, the excavator further estimated a total of more than ten thousand mummies." Four styles: ~60 with gilded masks and chest-pieces; cartonnage; pottery anthropoid coffins; plain linen. Found when a guard's donkey broke through into a tomb. Hawass: "it would seem that workshops were everywhere and artisans were one of the main professions in Baharyia". [ESTABLISHED; 10,000 is an estimate] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_the_Golden_Mummies ; [SNIPPET] https://www.guardians.net/hawass/Valley_of_the_Golden_Mummies.htm
- Depression ~370 km SW of Cairo, "only 94km long and 42km wide"; Egypt's main iron-ore field (El Gedida, Ghorabi); Cretaceous dinosaur beds. [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR: Rough Guide/Wikipedia text in a GitHub corpus] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahariya_Oasis

### 5c. Farafra and the White Desert

- White Desert National Park: ~3,000–3,010 km² of wind-carved chalk, ~45 km north of Farafra; Crystal Mountain (calcite ridge with a hole through it) ~120 km from Farafra on the Bahariya road; Cairo–Bahariya 4–5 h by road, then 4×4; nights near freezing Dec–Feb. ### 5d. Dakhla — Amheida (Trimithis) and Deir el-Hagar

- Amheida = Roman Trimithis; NYU excavations since 2001 (Columbia to 2008): a large multi-phase bath house, a "school of Greek letters", a fourth-century church among Egypt's earliest, the painted house of Serenos, a demolished temple of Thoth. [ESTABLISHED] [SNIPPET] https://isaw.nyu.edu/research/amheida ; https://arce.org/project/excavations-amheida/ ; https://www.nyu.edu/about/news-publications/news/2024/october/archeologists-unearth-clues-about-life-in-the-roman-empire--and-.html
- Deir el-Hagar, ~10 km west of al-Qasr: "probably first built as a small sanctuary in the reign of Nero, after which the decoration of the innermost room was finished in Vespasian's reign. Under Titus and Domitian, the temple was extended… at the same time that the larger temple of Thoth at Amheida was being built"; to Amun-Re, Mut, Khonsu and Seth, "Lord of the Oasis" — Seth's cult survived here long after its suppression in the Valley. [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] ISAW, *An Oasis City* ch. 1 http://dlib.nyu.edu/awdl/isaw/oasis-city/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_(deity)

### 5e. Kharga — Hibis and the Darb el-Arbain

- ~200 km west of the Nile, ~150 km long, the largest oasis. Temple of Hibis: begun under the 26th Dynasty (Psamtik II), decorated by Darius I; "the only structure in Egypt dating to the Saite-Persian period (664–404 BCE) which has come down to modern times in relatively good condition"; Theban triad; Darius shown greeting Egyptian gods. El-Bagawat: >200 mud-brick chapels, 3rd–7th c. AD. [ESTABLISHED] [SNIPPET] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Hibis ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharga_Oasis
- Hibis was structurally anchored by the firm Cintec after 1990s earthquake damage; their dating ("started in 558BC… completed… in 522BC") is a contractor's chronology. [ESTABLISHED works; dates DISPUTED] [MIRROR] Fitzwilliam "Cyberscribe" newsletter (GitHub FitzwilliamMuseum/egyptology, cyberscribe/cybernews05.html)
- Darb el-Arba'in: easternmost trans-Saharan caravan route, al-Fashir → Selima → Bir Natrun → Kharga → Asyut, used from the Old Kingdom. Wikipedia credits Herodotus with the "forty days"; the only forty-day passage checkable here (Hdt. 2.29, "you will disembark and make a journey by land of forty days; for in the Nile sharp rocks stand forth") is the Nile detour above Elephantine, so the attribution is loose. Cambyses' army route Thebes → Kharga (seven days) → toward Siwa makes Kharga its last confirmed waypoint. [ESTABLISHED route; Herodotus link DISPUTED] [SNIPPET] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darb_El_Arba%27%C4%ABn ; [TEXT] Hdt. 2.29, 3.26.

### 5f. Gilf Kebir, Wadi Sura, Zerzura

Mirror: Wikipedia "Gilf Kebir" (CulTex 232/background.md).

- Plateau "7,770 km2 (3,000 sq mi)"; "rises 300 m (980 ft) from the Libyan Desert floor"; SW corner of Egypt. First sighted 1910 (Harding-King); Prince Kamal el-Din "first realised the true size of the plateau" in 1925–26; Bagnold 1930; "The western side of the Gilf Kebir was explored in 1932 by the Clayton-Almásy Expedition, headed by Sir Robert East Clayton and Count László E. Almásy… by Gypsy Moth plane, by car, and on foot." [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilf_Kebir
- Cave of Swimmers: found by Almásy in October 1933 (Frobenius expedition) at Wadi Sura; hand-sized ochre swimmers, ~8,000–6,000 years old; Almásy's 1934 *Az Ismeretlen Szahara* argued for a wetter Sahara. Zerzura, the legendary "white city", was never found; the search produced the rock art instead. Almásy later ran Operation Salaam (1941–42), smuggling German agents across the desert into Egypt. [ESTABLISHED] [SNIPPET] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_of_Swimmers ; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/premium/article/stone-age-art-sahara-desert-gilf-kebir-oasis ; https://www.bradshawfoundation.com/africa/gilf_kebir_cave_of_swimmers/index.php ; [MIRROR] Grokipedia "László Almásy"
- Cave of Beasts (Foggini-Mestekawi, Wadi Sura II): "In January 2003, Zerzura Expeditions and Jacopo Foggini independently discovered a major new rock art site in the Western Gilf Kebir"; others say 2002; ~10 km from the Swimmers; ~8,000 figures including headless "beasts". An LRDG dispatch rider's bag lost in WWII was found on the plateau in 2007. [ESTABLISHED; 2002/2003 DISPUTED] [MIRROR] same; [SNIPPET] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_of_Beasts
- Access: "All expeditions require military and environmental permits, GPS, satellite phones, and complete self-sufficiency in water, fuel, food, and emergency supplies. Access is exclusively by 4×4 convoy, typically from Dakhla Oasis, roughly 350 kilometers to the northeast"; escorts picked up at Dakhla; "A typical trip lasts 7 to 14 days and covers 2,000 to 3,000 kilometers"; national park declared 2007. [ESTABLISHED, changeable] [SNIPPET] https://ecovoyager.com/blog/egypts-gilf-kebir-a-complete-travel-guide/ ; https://sahara-overland.com/tag/gilf-kebir/ ; https://fjexpeditions.com/expeditions/past/oct10/oct10.htm ; https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/8gw3etboeirk/

### 5g. Wadi al-Hitan (Whale Valley)

Mirror: UNEP-WCMC World Heritage datasheet (unepwcmc/world-heritage-datasets).

- "In the Western Desert 150 km southwest of Cairo and 80 km west of Faiyum in the Wadi el-Rayan Protected Area." "20,015 ha, with a 5,885 ha desert buffer zone". "2005: Inscribed on the World Heritage list under Natural Criterion viii." "379 fossil whales (179 catalogued) and 40 catalogued vertebrates". "The largest was Basilosaurus isis, which was up to 21 meters long, with well developed five-fingered flippers on the forelimbs and the quite unexpected presence of hind legs, feet, and toes." The beds "cover a vital evolutionary period of some 4 million years when these mammals evolved from land to sea-going animals" (~40–37 Ma, Tethys shore); "the only place in the world where numerous archaeocete skeletons can be seen in place". Visitors restricted "to prearranged guided tours along a prescribed trail either on foot or by camel"; "by 2008, 12,000 visitors were arriving each year". [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] http://world-heritage-datasheets.unep-wcmc.org/datasheet/output/site/wadi-al-hitan-whale-valley/
- On-site Fossils and Climate Change Museum opened 2016. [MEMORY — verify] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_al_Hitan

### 5h. Wadi Natrun

- "a 25 km long depression in the western desert half-way between Cairo and Alexandria where there are about a dozen saline lakes, two of which… provide natron, the sodium carbonate used by the Pharaohs for mummification." Four surviving monasteries (of a traditional ~700): St Macarius, St Bishoy, Deir al-Suryan, Deir al-Baramus; continuously inhabited since 4th–6th-century foundations; each ringed by a high wall with a keep against raids; on Egypt's UNESCO tentative list. [ESTABLISHED; "700" is tradition] [SNIPPET] https://www.worldheritagesite.org/tentative/id/1827 ; https://whc.unesco.org/fr/listesindicatives/1827/ ; https://www.experienceegypt.eg/en/attraction-details/715/explore-the-four-remaining-monasteries-of-wadi-al-natrun

## 6. Sinai and the east

### 6a. Serabit el-Khadim

- Turquoise-mining plateau in SW Sinai (c. 29.04 N, 33.46 E), Middle and New Kingdom; Hathor "Mistress of Turquoise" had a temple that "started simply as a cave where the goddess was worshiped and grew over the centuries as a portico, chambers, halls, courtyards, pylons and rooms were added". [ESTABLISHED] [SNIPPET] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serabit_el-Khadim ; https://egyptunitedtours.com/serabit-el-khadim/
- Proto-Sinaitic (older Wikipedia text): "These mines were worked by prisoners of war from southwest Asia who presumably spoke a West Semitic language… The Serabit el-Khadim inscriptions were found in a temple of Hathor, and appear to be votive texts. Despite a century of study, researchers can agree on the decipherment of only a single phrase, cracked in 1916 by Alan Gardiner: לבעלת l bʿlt (to the Lady) [baʿlat (Lady) being a title of Hathor…]". Petrie's 1904–05 season recorded ~10 inscriptions; the corpus is now ~30–40. [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Sinaitic_script ; [SNIPPET] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serabit_el-Khadim_proto-Sinaitic_inscriptions
- Dating is contested: c. 1850–1800 BC (Amenemhat III–IV) versus c. 1550–1500 BC; "the choice of one or the other date decides whether it is proto-Sinaitic or proto-Canaanite". [DISPUTED] [SNIPPET] https://www.academia.edu/96844295/THE_PROTO_SINAITIC_INSCRIPTIONS_AT_SERABIT_EL_KHADIM_IN_THEIR_ARCHAEOLOGICAL_CONTEXT_DATE_AND_FUNCTION ; https://www.thecollector.com/first-alphabet-proto-sinaitic/
- Sinai 345, the near-bilingual sphinx (Gardiner, Peet & Černý, *Inscriptions of Sinai* II, 1955): "Sphinx in red sandstone, 237 mm. in length over all. Now in the B.M. Sinai 112 (Amenemhat III) names "The brother of the prince of Retjenu Khebdedem" riding a donkey — a named Canaanite in the mining crews. [ESTABLISHED] [MIRROR: EES text (archive.org EXCMEM45) quoted in GitHub research file oratis/Markup]

## Film hooks (fact → fiction; ⟂ marks where the truth ends)

1. **The hollow mountain.** Abu Simbel sits inside a man-made hill: two reinforced-concrete domes carry the rock over 20-t reassembled blocks, finished September 1968. → The ASI uses the dome void as a hangar/relay; robots enter by the engineers' inspection door. ⟂ The real void is an engineering cavity, not an army's hangar; span/height need checking.
2. **The sun lock.** Twice a year the beam lights Amun, Ra-Horakhty and the king, never Ptah. → A timing key: the ASI's core boots only when the beam hits; Ptah's shadow is the kill-switch; the rumoured one-day drift since 1968 is the bug the scientists exploit. ⟂ The one-day shift is lore; Wikipedia says only "may not be as precise".
3. **Seti's dead-end tunnel.** KV17's 174-m passage descends toward nothing and stops mid-stair (Hawass, 2010). → The mummy knows why; the robots cut the last step and reach what the workmen were digging for. ⟂ Nothing was found; anything beyond is invented.
4. **KV5, the flooded labyrinth.** 120–150 rooms opposite Ramesses II's tomb, sealed by flash-flood debris, closed to visitors. → The robot base under the tourists' feet; a staged flash flood — the valley's real hazard — clears it. ⟂ ~7% cleared; its plan is unknown.
5. **The 1,090-tonne crack.** The Unfinished Obelisk is still attached to bedrock. → The robots finish it and raise it in a night, the largest single stone ever moved, the ASI's first "miracle". ⟂ Pick 1,090 or 1,168 t and stick to it.
6. **Cambyses in the sand.** Herodotus: the army left Kharga for Siwa and vanished in a south wind. → The robots' first find in the Great Sand Sea is Persian bronze; the mummy says Petubastis ambushed them. ⟂ Never found; every "discovery" so far is fringe or hoax.
7. **Shali, salt city.** A five-storey fortress of salt-mud brick that dissolved in three days of rain in 1926. → A dam release or rain-machine as siege weapon: water melts the walls. ⟂ Real chemistry, invented event.
8. **The alphabet's mine.** Canaanite miners at Serabit turned hieroglyphs into letters; only "to Baalat" is agreed. → The ASI's first message to humanity is cut in Proto-Sinaitic on the Hathor cave. ⟂ One phrase deciphered; the rest is invention.
9. **The single-channel choke.** Ever Given wedged at 30°00′57″N 32°34′45″E where the canal has one lane; $3 bn/day stopped for six days. → The robots seize that reach; the 35-km second lane is the way round. ⟂ Real geometry, fictional takeover.
10. **The second tomb.** Litherland publicly suspects Thutmose II's body lies in an undiscovered second tomb in the Western Wadis. → A real open dig as the third-act location. ⟂ Unconfirmed.
11. **Off-grid base.** Gilf Kebir: 7,770 km², no roads, 7–14 days by convoy, permits and escorts, Almásy's smuggling route. → Where the ASI builds unseen. ⟂ Real logistics, invented base.
12. **The library that never closed.** St Catherine's: 36 monks, 85 × 76 m granite walls, a 1,500-year library, a mosque inside. → The one archive the ASI cannot ingest because it was never digitised. ⟂ Much of it has been imaged (Sinai Palimpsests, UCLA).
