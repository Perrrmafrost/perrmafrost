## 1. The shabti spell — Book of the Dead chapter 6

- The spell is inscribed on the small mummiform figures (shabti / shawabti / ushebti) placed in tombs from the Middle Kingdom onward; it descends from Coffin Text spell 472. When the dead man is "called" to corvée labour in the afterlife, the figure answers for him. [ESTABLISHED] [SEARCH SNIPPET] https://www.worldhistory.org/article/119/shabti-dolls-the-workforce-in-the-afterlife/
- Faulkner's standard translation (as quoted by the pages above): "O shabti, allotted to me, if I be summoned or if I be detailed to do any work which has to be done in the realm of the dead; or if the unpleasant duty of a man at his task shall be imposed on me, you shall detail yourself for me on every occasion of planting the fields, of flooding the banks or of conveying sand from east to west; 'Here am I' you shall say." [ESTABLISHED] [SEARCH SNIPPET] https://thecuriousegyptologist.com/2021/04/02/the-shabti-a-helper-for-everyday-of-the-year/ ; https://www.worldhistory.org/article/119/shabti-dolls-the-workforce-in-the-afterlife/
- Renouf's 1904 translation, chapter VI, "Chapter whereby the funereal Statuettes may be made to do work for a person in the Netherworld": "O Statuette there! Should I be called and appointed to do any of the labours that are done in the Netherworld by a person according to his abilities, lo! all obstacles have been beaten down for thee; be thou counted for me at every moment, for planting the fields, for watering the soil, for conveying the sands of east and west. Here am I, whithersoever thou callest me." Renouf's note: the late word *ušebti* "has very naturally been considered as derived from … 'to answer.' For the statuette is addressed at the beginning of the chapter, and it replies at the end." [ESTABLISHED] [TEXT FETCHED] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GITenberg/Egyptian-Book-of-the-dead_69566/master/69566-0.txt (Gutenberg #69566)
- Budge 1920: "The text of Chapter VI was cut on figures made of stone, wood, etc. (ushabtiu), which were placed in the tomb, and when the deceased recited it these figures became alive and did everything he wished … About 700 ushabtiu figures were found in the tomb of Seti I, and many of them are in the British Museum." [ESTABLISHED] [TEXT FETCHED] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GITenberg/The-Book-of-the-Dead_7145/master/7145-8.txt (Gutenberg #7145)
- The canonical Late-Period set is **401 figures: 365 workers (one per day) + 36 overseers (one per ten-day "week")**. **Tutankhamun's tomb held 413: 365 workmen, 36 overseers and 12 supplementary foremen, one per month**, equipped with **1,866 miniature tools** (hoes, picks, yokes, baskets). [ESTABLISHED] [SEARCH SNIPPET] https://egypt-museum.com/ushabti-of-tutankhamun/ ; https://talesfromthetwolands.org/2020/02/01/here-am-i/ ; https://www.globalegyptianmuseum.org/detail.aspx?id=14815
- **TECH RE-READ:** a voice-activated servitor with a wake-phrase ("Here am I") and a hard-coded task list (plant, irrigate, haul); 365 + 36 + 12 is a shift roster, not a prayer. The film's robots' acknowledgment phrase is already written.

## 2. The Opening of the Mouth

- Purpose: to restore the senses (mouth, eyes, breath) to the mummy or a statue so it can eat, speak and see; attested from the Pyramid Texts to the Roman period; performed on statues and temples as well as corpses. [ESTABLISHED] [SEARCH SNIPPET] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opening_of_the_mouth_ceremony
- Tools: "a ritual adze, an arm shaped ritual censer, a spooned blade known as a peseshkaf, a serpent-head blade, and a variety of other amulets." The **peseshkef** is a fish-tail-shaped flint blade thought to derive from the knife used to cut a newborn's umbilical cord — the ceremony as re-birth. [ESTABLISHED] [SEARCH SNIPPET] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opening_of_the_mouth_ceremony ; https://worldhistoryedu.com/opening-of-the-mouth-ceremony-in-ancient-egypt/ ; British Museum model set EA 58404: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA58404
- The full New Kingdom liturgy runs to about 75 episodes (E. Otto, *Das ägyptische Mundöffnungsritual*, 1960). [ESTABLISHED] [NOT FETCHED — verify] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opening_of_the_mouth_ceremony
- Budge's 1914 summary of the Pyramid-age rite: "under the earliest dynasties this ceremony was performed on a statue of the king … The essential ceremony consisted in applying to the lips of the statue a curiously shaped instrument called the PESH KEF … In later times the Liturgy of Opening the Mouth was greatly enlarged and was called the Book of Opening the Mouth. The ceremonies were performed by the Kher-heb priest, the son of the deceased, and the priests and ministrants called Sameref, Sem, Smer, Am-as, Am-khent, and the assistants called Mesentiu." [ESTABLISHED] [TEXT FETCHED] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GITenberg/The-Literature-of-the-Ancient-Egyptians_15932/master/15932-0.txt (Gutenberg #15932)
- Sequence (Budge): censing and "Thou art pure" ×4; water; "Thou hast received thy head, and thy bones have been brought unto thee before Keb"; the **ninth episode** is a staged death-and-rebirth — a priest "wrapped in the skin of a bull, lay on a small bed and feigned death … came forth from the bull's skin, and sat up; this act symbolized the resurrection of the king in the form of a spirit-body (sāhu)"; then the son-priest: "I have come to embrace thee. I am thy son. I am Horus. I have pressed for thee thy mouth"; then "taking two instruments made of metal the priest went through the motion of cutting open the mouth and eyes of the statue, and said: 'I have opened thy mouth. I have opened thy two eyes. I have opened thy mouth with the instrument of Anpu. I have opened thy mouth with the Meskha instrument wherewith the mouth of the gods was opened … with the iron that cometh forth from Set, with the Meskha instrument of iron wherewith he opened the mouth of the gods shall the mouth of the Osiris be opened. And the Osiris shall walk and shall talk.'" The words "brought the Double of the king back to this earth to live in the statue, and each time they were repeated they renewed the life of the king in the Other World." [ESTABLISHED text; Budge's staging is his reconstruction] [TEXT FETCHED] same file.
- **Meskha = the Great Bear** (Budge glosses "Meskha (the Great Bear)" in Pyramid Text of Unas), i.e. the adze-shaped constellation Meskhetiu; the adze blade is "iron" (Egyptian *biꜢ*). See §12 for meteoritic iron. [ESTABLISHED] [TEXT FETCHED] same file.
- Book of the Dead chapter 23 (Renouf): "Let my mouth be opened by Ptah, and let the muzzles which are upon my mouth be loosed by the god of my domain. Then let Thoth come, full and equipped with Words of Power, and let him loose the muzzles of Sutu which are upon my mouth … Let my mouth be opened by Ptah with that instrument of steel wherewith he openeth the mouths of the gods." Renouf's note 2: "The word here translated 'steel' is [biꜢ], upon which see M. Devéria's dissertation, 'Le Fer et l'Aimant'" (iron and the magnet). [ESTABLISHED] [TEXT FETCHED] Renouf file above.
- Tutankhamun's burial chamber, north wall, shows his successor Ay in the leopard-skin of the sem-priest performing the Opening of the Mouth on the king as Osiris with the adze — the only royal tomb to depict the rite on the king himself. [ESTABLISHED] [SEARCH SNIPPET] https://egypt-museum.com/opening-of-the-mouth-ceremony-tutankhamun/
- **TECH RE-READ:** a boot sequence — purification (cleaning contacts), "thou hast received thy head" (reattaching the head unit), the umbilical knife (cutting the tether), the iron-from-the-sky adze touched to mouth and eyes (enabling I/O), the bull-skin sleep and sit-up (a test reboot), and a rule that the ritual must be repeated to "renew the life" (periodic re-initialisation). It works on statues, i.e. on non-biological bodies.

## 4. The components of the person

- Budge's 1899 summary (older terminology; modern Egyptology uses *ib*, *šwt*, *Ꜣḫ*, *sꜢḥ*): "The physical body of a man was called KHAT, a word which indicates something in which decay is inherent … Attached to the body in some remarkable way was the KA, or 'double,' of a man; it may be defined as an abstract individuality or personality which was endowed with all his characteristic attributes, and it possessed an absolutely independent existence … The offerings made in the tombs at all periods were intended for the nourishment of the KA … these men were known as 'KA priests.'" "The soul was called BA … The BA dwelt in the KA … frequently depicted … as a human-headed hawk … It had the power to leave the tomb … it could, however, and did, revisit the body in the tomb, and from certain texts it seems that it could re-animate it and hold converse with it. Like the heart AB it was, in some respects, the seat of life in man." "The spiritual intelligence, or spirit, of a man was called KHU [= Akh], and it seems to have taken form as a shining, luminous, intangible shape of the body." "SEKHEM … 'power' … his vital force or strength personified." "KHAIBIT or 'shadow' [= Sheut]." "Finally we may mention the REN, or 'name' of a man, as one of his most important constituent parts … any person, who effected the blotting out of a man's name was thought to have destroyed him also … a nameless being could not be introduced to the gods … if the deceased knew the names of divine beings, whether friends or foes, and could pronounce them, he at once obtained power over them, and was able to make them perform his will." Summary: "the entity of a man consisted of body, double, soul, heart, spiritual intelligence or spirit, power, shadow, and name." [ESTABLISHED, in dated vocabulary] [TEXT FETCHED] https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GITenberg/Egyptian-Ideas-of-the-Future-Life_11277/master/11277.txt (Gutenberg #11277)
- Budge 1920 on the *sahu*: Chapter IB "gave the sahu, or 'spirit-body,' power to enter the Tuat immediately after the burial of the material body." [TEXT FETCHED] Budge 1920 file. ## 5. Erasing the Ren — the name as kill-switch

- The Legend of Ra and Isis (Turin Papyrus 1993, 19th–20th Dynasty; Budge 1912 translation): the god "whose names by reason of their multitude are unknowable, for [even] the gods know them not." Isis, "who had the knowledge of words [of power]," makes a serpent from Ra's spittle and dust; Ra is bitten and says: "My father and my mother uttered my name, and [they] hid it in my body at my birth so that none of those who would use against me words of power might succeed in making their enchantments have dominion over me." Isis: "O my divine father, tell me thy name, for he who is able to pronounce his name liveth." Ra recites his titles ("I am the Being who openeth his eyes and the light cometh; I am the Being who shutteth his eyes and there is darkness … I am the maker of the hours and the creator of the days … I am Khepera in the morning, and Ra at … noon, and Temu in the evening"), but Isis: "Among the things which thou hast said unto me thy name hath not been mentioned." Ra: "I will allow myself to be searched through by Isis, and my name shall come forth from my body and go into hers." Then "the throne in the Boat of Millions of Years was empty." Budge's footnote: "Thus the god's own name became his most important talisman." ## 6. The Duat — Amduat, Book of Gates, Book of Caverns

- Budge 1920 on the Tuat: "it was located on the borders of the visible world, in the Outer Darkness … When Ani the scribe arrived there he said, 'What is this to which I have come? There is neither water nor air here, its depth is unfathomable, it is as dark as the darkest night, and men wander about here helplessly. A man cannot live here and be satisfied' (Chapter CLXXV). In the Tuat there was neither tree nor plant, for it was the 'land where nothing grew.'" Chapter 175 also promises "Thou shalt live for millions of millions of years, a life of millions of years." Budge names the two great guides as "the Book of Him that is in the Tuat [Amduat] and the Book of Gates" and dates their invented demon-names "between the XIIth and the XVIIIth dynasties." [ESTABLISHED] [TEXT FETCHED] Budge 1920 file.
- Renouf notes the Book of Gates on "the alabaster sarcophagus of Seti I in the Soane Museum" (London). [ESTABLISHED] [TEXT FETCHED] Renouf file.
- The Amduat ("What is in the Duat") divides the night into **12 hours**, each a region with named gates, guardians and a text; the earliest complete copy is in the tomb of Thutmose III (KV34, c. 1425 BC). Hour 4–5: the waterless desert of Sokar (Rosetau) where the barque must be towed and becomes a serpent; Hour 6: Ra's ba unites with his corpse (Osiris); Hour 7: Apophis is bound and cut by Isis and Seth/Selket while the Mehen serpent coils around Ra; Hour 12: the barque is pulled through the body of the serpent "Life of the Gods" from tail to mouth and Ra emerges reborn as the scarab Khepri. The Book of Gates has 12 gates each guarded by a fire-spitting serpent; the Book of Caverns (Osireion of Seti I; tomb of Ramesses VI) shows six sections of ovals ("caverns") holding mummiform beings and the punishment of enemies in cauldrons. [ESTABLISHED] [NOT FETCHED — verify] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amduat ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Gates ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Caverns ; https://thebanmappingproject.com/
- The hieroglyph for Duat is a five-pointed star inside a circle (Gardiner N15). [ESTABLISHED] [NOT FETCHED — verify] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duat
- **TECH RE-READ:** an airless, waterless, lightless facility of twelve sealed sectors with gate guardians and a single maintenance vehicle (the barque) that must traverse it every night or the sun does not come back. The "caverns" are storage ovals with mummiform occupants. The star-in-circle glyph is a buried node.

## 7. Apep/Apophis and the Bremner-Rhind "Book of Overthrowing Apep"

- Papyrus Bremner-Rhind, British Museum EA 10188, hieratic, from Thebes. Budge 1912: "about 16 ft. 8 in. in length, and is 9 1/4 in. in width … 21 columns of hieratic text … and 12 columns … written in long lines … The titles, catch-words, rubrics, names of Apep and his fiends, and a few other words, are written in red ink. There are two colophons; in the one we have a date, namely, the 'first day of the fourth month of the twelfth year of Pharaoh Alexander, the son of Alexander,' i.e., B.C. 311 [modern dating 306/305 BC], and in the other the name of the priest who either had the papyrus written, or appropriated it, namely, Nes-Menu, or Nes-Amsu." It contains the Songs of Isis and Nephthys, the Ritual of Sokar, and "the 'Book of overthrowing Apep, the Enemy of Ra, the Enemy of Un-Nefer' (i.e., Osiris) … a series of spells which were recited during the performance of certain prescribed ceremonies, with the object of preventing storms, and dispersing rain-clouds, and removing any obstacle, animate or inanimate, which could prevent the rising of the sun in the morning." [ESTABLISHED] [TEXT FETCHED] Budge Legends file. R. O. Faulkner published the transcription (1933) and translation (JEA 1936–37). [SEARCH SNIPPET] https://www.attalus.org/egypt/apep.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apophis ; https://research.britishmuseum.org/research/collection_online/collection_object_details.aspx?objectId=113969&partId=1

## 22. The last hieroglyph — Philae, 24 August 394 AD — and the closing of the temples

- The last dated hieroglyphic inscription is a graffito by the priest **Esmet-Akhom** on Hadrian's Gate at Philae, dated to the Birthday of Osiris in the year corresponding to **24 August AD 394**, with a figure of the Nubian god Mandulis; the last dated demotic graffito (also Philae) is AD 452; Justinian ordered the Philae temples closed c. ## Open questions (where the record actually stops)

- The Turin Canon's god-reign totals (13,420 / 23,200 years) are readings of a shattered papyrus; confirm the current Museo Egizio/Ryholt readings before quoting numbers.
- Whether *didi* in the Heavenly Cow is red ochre (modern consensus) or a plant (Budge's "mandrakes"): use ochre on screen, but the film can let the mummy correct the museum label.
- The exact chapter-titles and rubric wording of the Bremner-Rhind Apep ritual (Faulkner JEA 1936–37) were not read in primary form this session; the summary is second-hand.
- Whether the Opening of the Mouth adze blades found in tombs were ever actually of meteoritic iron (most surviving "adzes" are wood or copper models); the *texts* call the tool iron, the *objects* mostly do not.
- The date of the Memphite Theology's composition (Old Kingdom original vs. 25th-Dynasty archaising forgery) is genuinely unsettled; do not present it as "the oldest creation text."
- Herodotus' 70 days versus Egyptian sources (c. 40 days in natron within a 70-day whole): pick one and be consistent.
- Reserve heads: no consensus on function; the "kill the image" theory is the most cinematic and the least secure.
- The Philae graffito's exact date (24 Aug 394) and the Justinian closure (535–537) were not confirmed in this session; both are standard but should be checked against the Wikipedia/Procopius citations before use.
