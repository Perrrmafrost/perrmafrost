## PART A — THE GREAT PYRAMID: WHAT IS MEASURED

### A1. Baseline numbers (all [ESTABLISHED], [V-MIRROR] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza unless noted)
- Original height 280 royal cubits = 146.6–146.7 m (481 ft); current 138.5 m; base 440 cubits = 230.3 m square; volume ~2.6 million m³ including an internal bedrock hillock. Slope: seked of 5½ palms = 51°50'40". Tallest man-made structure for >3,800 years.
- ~2.3 million blocks, ~6 million tonnes: ~5.5 million t local nummulitic limestone (fossil shell disks visible; fossil shark teeth found in blocks), ~8,000 t Aswan granite, ~500,000 t mortar. Ancient name *Akhet Khufu*, "Khufu's Horizon". Built c. 2570 BC over about 27 years (Diary of Merer supports late-reign work; radiocarbon on mortar charcoal: 46 samples taken 1984/1995 calibrated 2871–2604 BC; reanalysis of younger samples gives completion 2620–2484 BC).
- Precision: the four base sides differ by an average of only 58 mm; corners squared to a mean error of 12 arc-seconds; the perimeter strip of bedrock is level to within 21 mm; casing joints average 0.5 mm. Petrie (1880–82) measured the lowest course at 148 cm high, top courses barely 50 cm; 203 courses survive. Sir David Gill's 1874 survey mast is still on the summit; his measurements were good to 1 mm.
- Casing: white Tura limestone ferried across the Nile from quarries ~10 km SE of Giza; stripped after 1356 (Sultan an-Nasir Hasan) and again by Muhammad Ali Pasha for the Alabaster Mosque; a few casing stones survive in situ on the north face, excavated by Vyse in 1837. Claims that the casing was "cast in place" are refuted by petrography.
- Granite: transported from Aswan "more than 900 km south" (the film brief says ~800 km; Wikipedia's figure is >900 km by river). Largest blocks 25–80 t form the King's Chamber ceiling and the five Relieving Chambers above it; the King's Chamber ceiling is nine slabs totalling ~400 t; every roof beam is cracked because the chamber settled 2.5–5 cm. The chamber is 20 × 10 cubits (10.5 × 5.2 m), granite-lined, uninscribed (the 4th-Dynasty norm). Its sarcophagus (external 228 × 98 × 105 cm; walls 15 cm) shows copper-saw and tubular-drill marks and is too big to have come round the Ascending/Descending Passage corner, so it was placed before the roof went on.
- Pyramidion lost in antiquity; Pliny and later authors describe a platform on top; the summit is ~8 m short, ~1,000 t missing. All known 4th-Dynasty pyramidia are plain white limestone, not gilded; gilded capstones are attested only from the 5th Dynasty ("white gold pyramidion of the pyramid Sahure's Soul Shines").

### A2. Alignment to true north — settled facts, unsettled method
- The base sides deviate from the geographic (not magnetic) cardinal directions by an average of 3 minutes 38 seconds of arc, about a tenth of a degree. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia]
- Kate Spence, "Ancient Egyptian chronology and the astronomical orientation of pyramids," *Nature* 408: 320–324 (2000), doi 10.1038/35042510: alignment by the simultaneous transit of two circumpolar stars (Kochab in Ursa Minor and Mizar in Ursa Major) with a plumb line; because precession slowly rotates the line joining them, the residual errors of successive pyramids would date their foundation. Criticized by Rawlins & Pickering (2001) and others; not consensus. [DISPUTED] [V-SECONDARY: sandner-art/SC-Ancient-Cultures; AlienLifeShop/TheoriesOfAnything, both citing the Nature paper]
- Glen Dash, "Occam's Egyptian razor: the equinox and the alignment of the pyramids," *Journal of Ancient Egyptian Architecture* 2 (2017) (Live Science coverage: "Secret to Great Pyramid's Near Perfect Alignment Possibly Found"); earlier "New Angles on the Great Pyramid," *AERAGRAM* 16.2 (2015): 8–13. Dash's field experiment: on the autumn equinox, track the tip of a rod's (gnomon's) shadow through the day; the points fall on an almost straight east–west line. The method reproduces not only the accuracy but the *direction* of the observed error: Khufu's, Khafre's and Sneferu's Red Pyramid are all rotated slightly counter-clockwise from true north by similar amounts. Dash's own caveat: the Egyptians left no record of which method they used, so the equinox method is the simplest hypothesis, not a proven one. [DISPUTED (method) / ESTABLISHED (the counter-clockwise error pattern)] [V-SECONDARY sandner-art; luvor/grimoire footnote; details MEMORY: http://www.egyptian-architecture.com/JAEA2/JAEA2_Dash.pdf]
- Other proposed methods: bisecting the rising and setting points of a circumpolar star on an artificial horizon; the "Indian circle" solar shadow method. No consensus; all are within Old Kingdom capability. [V-SECONDARY AlienLifeShop archaeoastronomy file]
- Leveling method is also not settled: Lehner suggests bedrock post-holes around the base held alignment stakes; I.E.S. Edwards proposed water-filled trenches, "although it is unclear how practical and workable such a system would be." [V-MIRROR Wikipedia]

### A3. The concavity — the "eight-sided" pyramid
- Petrie noted in 1880 that the sides "are very distinctly hollowed" and "each side has a sort of groove specially down the middle of the face", which he attributed to thicker casing there. A 2005 laser-scan survey confirmed the anomalies, attributable "to some degree" to damaged and removed stones. Under certain lighting (equinox dawn/dusk) and image enhancement the faces appear split, hence speculation that the pyramid was deliberately built eight-sided. [ESTABLISHED (the indentation exists; ~1 m at the centre of each face) / DISPUTED (intentional design vs casing-loss artefact)] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia]
- The famous photograph is a 1940 aerial by RAF pilot P. Groves, published by André Pochan. [MEMORY] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza#Concavity
- Pi: perimeter/height = 1760/280 cubits = 2π to better than 0.05% (i.e., 22/7). Verner: "although the ancient Egyptians could not precisely define the value of π, in practice they used it." Petrie: "these relations of areas and of circular ratio are so systematic that we should grant that they were in the builder's design." Counter-view: the slope follows from choosing a seked of 5½ palms alone, with no π intended. [DISPUTED] [V-MIRROR]

### A4. The 1837 iron plate (J.R. Hill)
- Found in May 1837 by J.R. Hill, an engineer working for Col. Howard Vyse, while blasting the outer end of the southern King's Chamber "air shaft" with gunpowder; a flat wrought-iron plate roughly 26 × 8.6 cm and a few mm thick, sworn by Hill and others to have been embedded in an inner joint of the masonry. Now in the British Museum. [MEMORY] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza (section on the iron plate); https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA2433
- El Sayed El Gayar and M. P. Jones, "Metallurgical investigation of an iron plate found in 1837 in the Great Pyramid at Gizeh, Egypt," *Journal of the Historical Metallurgy Society* 23 (1989): they concluded it was ancient smelted (not meteoritic — nickel too low) iron of Old Kingdom date, with traces of gold on one surface, perhaps once gilded. [V-SECONDARY: lerwang/en mirror of Wikipedia text for the citation; conclusions MEMORY]
- Paul Craddock and Janet Lang of the British Museum (1993) re-examined it and argued the metallography is consistent with much later (post-Roman) iron and that the plate could be intrusive. Its date remains contested; it is NOT accepted as proof of Old Kingdom iron-working, though tiny iron objects (mostly meteoritic) are known from Predynastic and Old Kingdom contexts. [DISPUTED] [MEMORY]
- For the film: the plate is real, sits in the British Museum, and nobody can agree what century it belongs to — a perfect object for the mummy to "identify".

### A5. Shafts, doors and robots (Queen's Chamber)
- 1872: Waynman Dixon opens the Queen's Chamber shafts by chiselling into the walls. Finds: a diorite ball, a bronze hook (both British Museum) and a cedar plank. The plank passed to James Grant, was donated to Aberdeen in 1946, mis-filed, rediscovered 2020 and radiocarbon-dated to 3341–3094 BC — over 500 years older than Khufu; curator Abeer Eladany suggests heartwood of a long-lived tree or recycled timber (Curtis & Eladany 2022, doi 10.11588/cipeg.2020.4.86467). [ESTABLISHED (date) / DISPUTED (meaning)] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia; V-SECONDARY audit]
- 1993: Rudolf Gantenbrink's crawler robot *Upuaut 2* climbs ~65 m up the southern Queen's Chamber shaft and finds a limestone "door" with two eroded copper "handles". Sept 2002: National Geographic's Pyramid Rover drills a small hole and finds another slab behind; the northern shaft is also blocked by a slab. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR]
- 2011: the Djedi Project (University of Leeds/Prof. Rob Richardson; Scoutek Ltd/Shaun Whitehead; sponsor Dassault Systèmes; SCA/Hawass). A 5 kg inch-worm robot travelled 63.6 ± 0.4 m up the 20 × 20 cm shaft at 40°; a <8 mm "micro snake camera" went through the 2002 hole and saw all sides of the ~23 × 19 cm cavity behind the first slab; red ochre marks on the floor (undeciphered by the team; mathematician Luca Miatello reads them as "121", the shaft's length in cubits); the copper "handles" loop back on themselves and look decorative; the back of the "door" is finished and polished, "which suggests that it was not put there just to block the shaft from debris." A mason's red mark was also seen on the shaft wall. Planned improvements (bigger camera, sonic thickness gauge on the second slab, northern shaft) were never realised after the 2011 revolution. Richardson et al., *Journal of Field Robotics* 30(3): 323–348 (2013), doi 10.1002/rob.21451. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia; V-SECONDARY popojan/orbit notes summarising the paper]
- King's Chamber shafts: 18–21 cm wide, 14 cm high; the southern rises at 45°; the northern dog-legs west "perhaps to avoid the Big Void"; one unfinished ceiling stone Gantenbrink nicknamed the "Monday morning block". The star-shaft idea is largely dismissed because of the bends; the ventilation idea is also abandoned in favour of a ritual "ascent of the king's spirit". [V-MIRROR]

### A6. Attribution, Vyse and the "forged cartouche"
- Four upper Relieving Chambers were opened in 1837 by Howard Vyse with gunpowder and boring rods (the lowest, Davison's, was known since 1763). Their walls carry red-ochre work-gang graffiti, e.g. "The gang, The white crown of Khnum-Khufu is powerful"; Khufu's names appear over a dozen times. The graffiti are "often side-ways or upside down, and their sometimes being partially covered by blocks, seems to indicate that the stones were inscribed before being laid", and the same gang-name formulae occur at Hatnub, Wadi al-Jarf and in the pyramids of Menkaure and Sahure. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia]
- Fringe claim: Zecharia Sitchin (*The Stairway to Heaven*, 1980) alleged Vyse forged the cartouches; Scott Creighton's *The Great Pyramid Hoax* (2016) revives it, arguing the cartouche orientation "contradicts ancient Egyptian writing convention". Mainstream reply: paint runs into joints inaccessible after construction, the palaeography was only correctly read decades later (Vyse could not have known the forms), and the Merer papyri and Giza mastaba inscriptions independently name Akhet-Khufu. [HOAX claim; the graffiti themselves are ESTABLISHED] [V-SECONDARY: techidaily mirror of the Creighton blurb; V-MIRROR Wikipedia for the rebuttal]
- Also relevant: Amenhotep II's stela (found by Hassan 1936) shows the New Kingdom still attributed the two big pyramids to Khufu and Khafre; Djedefre's cartouche is on the boat-pit cover blocks; Hetepheres I's intact but empty coffin lies 110 m east. [V-MIRROR]

## PART B — SCANPYRAMIDS AND THE NEW GEOPHYSICS

### B1. How muography works (one paragraph, for the screenplay)
Cosmic rays hitting the upper atmosphere produce muons — heavy cousins of the electron — which rain down at roughly one per square centimetre per minute (about 10,000 per m² per minute; "a single muon passes through an area the size of a human hand per second"). They penetrate hundreds of metres of rock, but the more mass they cross, the more are absorbed. A detector left inside or beside a structure records the arrival direction of each muon for weeks or months; a direction from which *more* muons arrive than the solid-stone model predicts means there is less matter that way — a void. Three detector families were used at Giza: nuclear-emulsion films (photographic plates that record each track; Nagoya University), plastic scintillator hodoscopes (KEK, Japan) and gas detectors/Micromegas (CEA, France). The technique dates to E. P. George (1950s, tunnel overburden in Australia); Luis Alvarez's team used spark chambers in Khafre's lower chamber in 1966–69 to hunt hidden rooms ("Search for Hidden Chambers in the Pyramids," *Science* 167: 832–839, 1970) and found none. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia "Muon tomography" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muon_tomography ; V-SECONDARY for the Alvarez citation; CERN Bulletin 50/2010 mirrored]

### B2. ScanPyramids (2015– )
- Launched October 2015 under the Egyptian Ministry of Antiquities; run by Cairo University's Faculty of Engineering and the French HIP Institute, with Nagoya University, KEK, CEA and later TU Munich. Methods: infrared thermography, muography, GPR, ERT, photogrammetry. [ESTABLISHED] [V-SECONDARY biondi write-up; V-MIRROR Wikipedia] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ScanPyramids
- **The Big Void (Nov 2017).** Morishima et al., "Discovery of a big void in Khufu's Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons," *Nature* 552: 386–390 (2017), doi 10.1038/nature24647. A cavity at least 30 m long, with a cross-section similar to the Grand Gallery's, lying above the Grand Gallery. Detected first with Nagoya's emulsion films in the Queen's Chamber, then confirmed by KEK's scintillator hodoscopes in the same chamber and by CEA's gas detectors placed outside the pyramid — three independent technologies (the emulsion result exceeded 5σ). The team stated the void "is completely different from previously identified construction spaces"; Zahi Hawass suggested it may be a construction gap left when building the Gallery. Its purpose is unknown and it is not accessible. A Kyushu/Tohoku/Tokyo/Chiba team planned a 2020 rescan with a new detector; COVID delayed it. Whether the void is horizontal or inclined, one space or several, is not resolved. [ESTABLISHED (existence) / OPEN (purpose)] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia; V-SECONDARY audit + AlienLifeShop muon file]
- **The North Face Corridor.** First seen as a muon anomaly behind the entrance chevrons in 2016; confirmed 2019 as a corridor at least 5 m long, horizontal or sloping up (not parallel to the Descending Passage). Procureur et al., "Precise characterization of a corridor-shaped structure in Khufu's Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons," *Nature Communications* 14 (2023), doi 10.1038/s41467-023-36351-0: ~9 m long, ~2 × 2 m section. On 2 March 2023 the Ministry showed endoscope video taken through a joint in the chevrons: a horizontal, unfinished-looking tunnel 9 m long, ~2.1 m wide, with a chevron (gabled) ceiling like the ones over the original entrance and the top Relieving Chamber. The parallel NDT study is Elkarmoty et al., "Localization and shape determination of a hidden corridor in the Great Pyramid of Giza using non-destructive testing," *NDT & E International* 139 (2023) 102809 (GPR + ultrasonic). [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia for dimensions/date; V-SECONDARY audit for DOI; Elkarmoty citation MEMORY: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ndteint.2023.102809]
- Purpose of the corridor: unknown. Leading guesses: a weight-distributing structure over the entrance (like the Relieving Chambers), or an access to something else (its west end lies over the Descending Passage). Hawass floated a connection to Khufu's "real" burial; ScanPyramids did not. [OPEN]
- **2025 independent confirmations.** Abdel-Maksoud et al. 2025, *Scientific Reports*, doi 10.1038/s41598-025-91115-8: fused GPR, ultrasound and ERT confirm the corridor without muons. Pugacheva et al. 2025, *Scientific Reports*, doi 10.1038/s41598-025-29081-4: ERT alone detects it and quantifies how much resistivity over-estimates its size. Abdel-Maksoud et al. 2025, *NDT & E International*, doi 10.1016/j.ndteint.2025.103331: two shallow anomalies behind an unusually polished patch on Menkaure's east face (a possible second entrance — anomalies only, no entrance shown). [ESTABLISHED] [V-SECONDARY audit]
- A separate muon project, ScIDEP, is building scintillator telescopes to scan Khafre's pyramid from its burial chamber (Alrheli et al. 2025, arXiv:2503.21959) — planned, no results yet. Do not confuse with the SAR "underground city" (B4). [V-SECONDARY audit]

### B3. The Western Cemetery anomaly (2024)
- Sato Motoyuki et al., *Archaeological Prospection* (2024), doi 10.1002/arp.1940 (Higashi Nippon International University / Tohoku University with Egypt's NRIAG [team composition MEMORY]). GPR imaged a backfilled, L-shaped feature roughly 10 × 10 m and up to ~2 m deep in the Western Cemetery, in a blank area with no visible mastabas; ERT found a resistive (low-conductivity) anomaly beneath it at ~5–10 m. The authors state that neither method can identify the deeper material or prove it is a void; interpretations range from a filled entrance to a shaft tomb, gravel or a natural feature. ### B5. Water, ramps and the construction question (recent peer-reviewed work)
- Sheisha et al. 2022, *PNAS*, doi 10.1073/pnas.2202530119: five floodplain cores + pollen reconstruct the "Khufu branch" of the Nile beside Giza; high water during Khufu–Menkaure made river access to the plateau feasible; radiocarbon-modelled Khufu accession 2613–2577 BC (1σ). Ghoneim et al. 2024, *Communications Earth & Environment*, doi 10.1038/s43247-024-01379-7: a buried 64 km "Ahramat Branch" runs past 31 pyramid complexes from Lisht to Giza; causeways and valley temples end at it. Younes et al. 2024, *Geology*, doi 10.1130/G51965.1: copper pollution in Khufu's harbour sediments peaks ~2615 ± 80 to 2485 ± 80 BC — a chemical fingerprint of the tool economy. [ESTABLISHED] [V-SECONDARY audit]
- Ramps: Diodorus says ramps were used and removed; a 2018 excavation at the Hatnub alabaster quarry exposed an Old Kingdom central ramp flanked by staircases and post-holes for rope teams (an analogue, not a pyramid ramp). Houdin's internal-spiral-ramp theory (2006/2008, with Bob Brier; Dassault 3D modelling) is unconfirmed; Scheuring 2025, *npj Heritage Science*, doi 10.1038/s40494-025-02018-w models sliding ramps and counterweights (no surviving hardware); Rosell Roig 2026, doi 10.1038/s40494-026-02405-x models four edge ramps. Landreau et al. 2024, *PLOS ONE*, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0306690 proposes a hydraulic lift inside Djoser's Step Pyramid (interpretation, not excavated mechanism); Landreau et al. 2026, doi 10.1038/s40494-026-02833-9 reinterprets two 600–700 m walls at Dahshur as possible dams near the Red Pyramid (undated, in a military zone). **The exact ramp/lifting method for the Great Pyramid remains genuinely unknown.** [DISPUTED] [V-SECONDARY audit; V-MIRROR Wikipedia]
- Quarrying rate: Franck Burgos's experiment in a Khufu quarry found in 2017 (arsenical-copper chisels, wooden mallets, ropes): four men cut a 2.5 t block in four 6-hour days, six times faster when the stone was wetted; ~3,500 quarrymen could deliver the 250 blocks/day needed for a 27-year build. A 1999 construction-management study with Lehner: average workforce ~13,200, peak ~40,000. Herodotus's 100,000 is rhetoric. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR]
- Erdil et al. 2025, *PLOS ONE*, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0314612: Bayesian model of 160 radiocarbon dates favours the "High" Old Kingdom chronology — dates in popular use may be decades to a century too young. [V-SECONDARY audit]

## PART C — TEXTS AND TOWNS THAT SETTLED THE "WHO BUILT IT" QUESTION

### C1. Wadi al-Jarf and the Diary of Merer
- Wadi al-Jarf is a Red Sea harbour of Khufu (turquoise/copper route to Sinai; the opposite-shore fort Tell Ras Budran). First noticed 1823 (Wilkinson & Burton), re-dug 1954 (Bissey & Chabot-Morisseau, stopped by Suez 1956), re-excavated from June 2011 by Pierre Tallet and Gregory Marouard (IFAO/Sorbonne). In 2013 hundreds of papyrus fragments were found in storage galleries — the oldest inscribed papyri known. Ten are well preserved; most date to Khufu's year 26/27 (the "year after the 13th cattle count"), addressed to the living king by Horus name. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia "Khufu" and "Great Pyramid"]
- Papyri Jarf A and B are the logbook of the inspector (*sḥḏ*) Merer, leader of a *za* (phyle) of ~40 men in four "Overseer of Ten" units, ferrying white Tura limestone to *Akhet-Khufu* (written with the pyramid determinative, dozens of times), accepting stones at *She Akhet-Khufu* ("the pool of the pyramid Horizon of Khufu") and *Ro-She Khufu* ("the entrance to the pool of Khufu"), under the vizier **Ankhhaf**, Khufu's half-brother and owner of the largest mastaba in the Giza East Field (G 7510). Rhythm: two or three round trips every ten days, ~30 blocks of 2–3 t per trip, ~200 blocks a month; Tallet thinks these were casing stones for the final phase. Tallet's edition: *Les papyrus de la mer Rouge I* (MIFAO 136, 2017; English translation PDF https://www.ifao.egnet.net/uploads/publications/divers/MIFAO136_ann_01.pdf ); Tallet & Lehner, *The Red Sea Scrolls* (Thames & Hudson 2022); a second volume, MIFAO 145 (2025), adds the logbooks of Dedi's teams (Papyrus D: supply and guard duty, probably at Khufu's valley temple and perhaps a palace at the foot of the complex). The papyri are in the Egyptian Museum, Cairo [MEMORY for current location]. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR; V-SECONDARY chiefautism/great-awakening-map (citing Smithsonian, Archaeology Magazine, HISTORY); audit]

### C2. The workers' town (Heit el-Ghurab, "Lost City of the Pyramid Builders")
- South of the Sphinx behind the cyclopean Wall of the Crow; excavated by Mark Lehner/AERA since 1988–89. Grid-planned "galleries" (long barracks with a colonnaded front, a sleeping platform, a hearth/kitchen at the south end; ~2,000 people could be housed), bakeries by the dozen with bread moulds, breweries, copper workshops, a "Royal Administrative Building" with granary silos, a hypostyle hall. Faunal remains: huge quantities of young cattle, sheep and goat — prime beef, "food a slave gang would never have seen"; pottery-mound analysis suggests higher-status areas ate more cattle. Dated by pottery, sealings (Khafre and Menkaure names) and stratigraphy to Khafre (2520–2494 BC) and Menkaure (2490–2472 BC). Lehner later suggested the galleries may have housed soldiers/sailors for a harbour, with pyramid gangs perhaps camping on the ramps or in quarries. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia "Giza pyramid complex", "Great Pyramid"; V-SECONDARY msu-anthropology/daea (citing Lehner 2011 and OI reports); markberning/timeline-v2]
- The workers' cemetery south of the Wall of the Crow (found by Hawass, 1990; used from Khufu to the end of the 5th Dynasty) contains tombs of overseers and labourers with healed fractures and titles; the "built by slaves" idea comes from Herodotus and Diodorus, not from Egyptian evidence. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR]

### C3. Khufu's solar boat
- Two sealed pits south of the pyramid, still covered with slabs up to 15 t, were found in May 1954 by Kamal el-Mallakh. The eastern pit held 1,224 pieces of Lebanese cedar (longest 23 m, shortest 10 cm) laid in 13 layers; Haj Ahmed Youssef Moustafa spent 14 years (some sources say ~10) working out the "shell-first" construction: unpegged tenons of Christ's-thorn, planks lashed with halfa grass, no keel. Result: 43.4–43.6 m long, 5.9 m wide, 1.78 m deep — the world's oldest intact ship, "a masterpiece of woodcraft" that could sail today. Shown from 1982 in a boat-shaped museum beside the pyramid; moved 7.5 km in a special vehicle to the Grand Egyptian Museum in August 2021; the old museum was demolished. The second pit was probed by camera in 1987 and excavated from 2011 by Waseda University (Sakuji Yoshimura); its boat is being reassembled at GEM. Function debated: ritual solar barque vs funerary barge that carried the body from Memphis (it shows signs of use in water). ## PART D — UNDERGROUND GIZA: THE OSIRIS SHAFT AND THE SPHINX

### D1. The Osiris Shaft (the "Water Shaft")
- A vertical shaft under Khafre's causeway, midway between the Sphinx and Khafre's pyramid, leading to three levels; the lowest is flooded by groundwater. Recorded by Selim Hassan in the 1930s (he could not fully explore it because of water); pumped out and fully excavated by Zahi Hawass in 1999 (spurred by Hall-of-Records enthusiasts); Hawass published "The Discovery of the Osiris Shaft at Giza" in *Essays in Honor of David B. O'Connor* (ASAE Cahier 36, 2007) pp. 379–397; opened to tourists November 2017. Dated to the Late Period (Saite–Persian, c. 664–332 BC) with reuse; total depth ~30 m. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia "Giza pyramid complex" and "Hall of Records"; V-SECONDARY artshumrc/giza publication list; biondi write-up]
- Layout (from Hawass's report): level 1 an empty chamber ~10 m down; level 2 (~20 m) a hall with six or seven side niches, two holding huge basalt/granite sarcophagi (one with bones and Late Period pottery/ushebtis); level 3 (~30 m) a chamber whose floor is cut into a channel of water surrounding a central "island" on which a sarcophagus lid and the remains of four pillars rest — hence Hawass's reading as a symbolic tomb of Osiris and his link to Herodotus's "island". Small ~40 × 40 cm tunnels lead off toward the Great Pyramid and the Sphinx and are unexplored. [MEMORY for level-by-level detail; V-SECONDARY for the tunnels] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osiris_Shaft
- Earlier "hidden chamber" scans: 1978 SRI International GPR near the Sphinx's paws (Edgar Cayce/ARE-funded) found only natural fissures on drilling; 1996–98 Schor/Florida State survey; in 1998 the SCA allowed one test drill near the Great Pyramid, which hit a natural cavity, after which permission was withdrawn. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia "Hall of Records"]

### D2. Sphinx water-erosion hypothesis
- Mainstream: the Sphinx is carved from the Mokattam limestone of the plateau (alternating hard and soft beds) in the reign of Khafre, c. 2500 BC, as part of his complex (Sphinx Temple, causeway, Valley Temple built from blocks quarried out of the Sphinx enclosure); Amenhotep II later built a temple to it as Hauron-Haremakhet. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia "Giza pyramid complex"; V-SECONDARY AlienLifeShop]
- Fringe/heterodox: R. A. Schwaller de Lubicz first suggested water weathering; John Anthony West recruited Boston University geologist Robert Schoch, who after fieldwork in 1990–91 argued the enclosure walls show deep vertical fissures with rounded profiles, the signature of rainfall runoff rather than wind-sand abrasion (which produces horizontal banding), and that the core body was carved c. 7000–5000 BC (he now argues for the end of the Ice Age, c. 10,000 BC, adding seismic-refraction weathering depths). Publicised by the 1993 NBC documentary *The Mystery of the Sphinx* (Charlton Heston). [FRINGE] [V-SECONDARY tshuldberg; AlienLifeShop; CulTex mirror of Wikipedia "The Mystery of the Sphinx"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphinx_water_erosion_hypothesis ; https://www.robertschoch.com/sphinx.html]
- Replies: Mark Lehner and geologist James Harrell attribute the profile to chemical weathering, salt crystallisation (K. Lal Gauri's haloclasty: the porous limestone wicks moisture, salts crystallise and flake the surface) and subsurface moisture; rainfall was heavier through the Old Kingdom to c. 2200 BC anyway; the softer beds weather faster whatever the agent; and no artefacts of a 7000 BC monumental culture exist at Giza. Geologist Colin Reader, sympathetic to an older Sphinx, proposes only a modest revision (Early Dynastic, before Khafre) from runoff patterns of the quarry to the west. [ESTABLISHED consensus; Reader's variant DISPUTED] [V-SECONDARY tshuldberg; MEMORY for Gauri and Reader specifics]

## PART E — SAQQARA, DAHSHUR, HAWARA, THE MUSEUM

### E1. The Serapeum of Saqqara
- Burial catacomb of the Apis bulls (living incarnations of Ptah; on death Osiris-Apis → Serapis). Auguste Mariette found a sphinx head in the dunes in 1850, followed the avenue, and entered the catacombs in November 1851; he found two undisturbed Apis burials (isolated tombs; three undisturbed burials by 1852 in chambers E and G) and thousands of stelae whose dates anchored 19th-century Egyptian chronology. Most of his notes were lost in the 1878 Boulaq flood; Maspero published one volume of *Le Sérapéum de Memphis* in 1882. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serapeum_of_Saqqara]
- Lesser Vaults: dug under Khaemweset, son of Ramesses II; Greater Vaults begun under Psamtik I (664–610 BC) and extended in the Ptolemaic period to ~350 m with a parallel service tunnel. From Amasis II to the end of the Ptolemies the sarcophagi were of hard stone (Aswan granite, "grey granite", diorite, limestone), "weighing as much as 62 tonnes each, including the lid" (older Wikipedia text: ~40 t box, 25 t lid). 24 sarcophagi survive; only four are inscribed — Amasis II (red granite, c. 550 BC, panelled with Pyramid-Text spells coloured green), Cambyses II (c. 530 BC, blocking the original entrance), Khabash (year 2, c. 336 BC) and one with empty cartouches (Ptolemy XII or Cleopatra?). Every tomb but two was plundered; bull mummies torn up, names scratched from stelae. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR]
- Logistics are documented, not mysterious: Mariette found roller tracks and two eight-lever wooden winches; Brugsch (1853) saw the "double-rails" still on the Ptolemaic tunnel floor; chambers were filled with sand then emptied to lower the boxes; a stela records 28 working days to bring one sarcophagus and lid into its chamber in year 37 of Ptolemy II (c. 247 BC); the last box was abandoned in the service tunnel. Edward VII lunched in a sarcophagus in 1862. Closed after the 1992 earthquake, stabilised 2001–2012, reopened 2012; work on the collapsed Lesser Vaults resumed 2020. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR]
- Fringe: Christopher Dunn (*Lost Technologies of Ancient Egypt*, 2010) claims interior flatness of "2–5 thousandths of an inch" requiring machine tools; his measurements were made with a straightedge and light, not metrology gear; Denys Stocks' experiments (*Experiments in Egyptian Archaeology*, 2003) show copper saws/tube drills with quartz sand and grinding stones reach these finishes with labour. [FRINGE claim / ESTABLISHED rebuttal] [V-SECONDARY AlienLifeShop; McWut-src/AncientAnomalies]
- Mariette's often-quoted anecdote of finding the embalmer's footprints in the dust of a sealed Apis chamber is in his own account; I could not fetch the text — treat the wording as [MEMORY].

### E2. Meidum, the Bent Pyramid and the Red Pyramid (Sneferu's learning curve)
- Meidum: begun as a step pyramid, converted to a true pyramid; the outer casing rested on sand, not rock, and slumped, leaving today's tower of core masonry in a rubble skirt; whether it collapsed during construction (Mendelssohn 1974) or centuries later is disputed. [ESTABLISHED/DISPUTED] [V-MIRROR CulTex mirrors of Wikipedia; animesh88 mirror of World History Encyclopedia]
- Bent Pyramid, Dahshur: rises at ~54° then, above 47 m, at 43°; probably a stability correction (Mendelssohn linked it to Meidum's failure; others cite ramp geometry above ~52°); it is unique in keeping most of its polished casing, which engineer Peter James credits to wider joints acting as thermal expansion gaps. Ancient name "The Southern Shining Pyramid". Reopened to tourists July 2019 (first time since 1965) via a 79 m tunnel; a western entrance leads to a second, higher chamber — two independent passage systems, unique in Egypt. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR]
- Red (North) Pyramid: 105 m high, 220 m base, uniform 43°, begun ~year 30 of Sneferu (c. 2590 BC); quarry-mark dates give a build of ~17 years (Stadelmann) or 10–11 years (Krauss, Romer); a fragmentary pyramidion reassembled on site (its angle does not match the pyramid). ## PART F — EGYPTIAN MATHEMATICS AND INSTRUMENTS

- **Rhind Mathematical Papyrus** (British Museum EA 10057/10058): bought by Alexander Henry Rhind in Luxor in 1858 (from illegal digging near the Ramesseum); c. 1650 BC (Second Intermediate Period), copied by the scribe Ahmes from a lost Middle Kingdom original of Amenemhat III's reign; 84 problems (the missing middle section surfaced in New York after 1922). Its final five problems concern pyramid slope. Problem 56: "If a pyramid is 250 cubits high and the side of its base 360 cubits long, what is its seked?" (answer 5 palms 1 finger). The *seked* is the horizontal run in palms (and fingers) per one cubit of vertical rise, i.e. the reciprocal of slope; a royal cubit = 7 palms = 28 fingers ≈ 52.4 cm. The Great Pyramid's seked of 5½ palms gives its 51°50' face; problem 60's steep little "pillar" has a seked of 4 palms. [ESTABLISHED] [V-MIRROR Wikipedia "Rhind Mathematical Papyrus" and "Ancient Egyptian mathematics" mirrors in ajb2969, miquelvir, gianmarcopicarella] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhind_Mathematical_Papyrus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seked
- **Merkhet and bay.** The *bay* is a palm rib with a V-slit at one end used as a sighting instrument; the *merkhet* ("instrument of knowing") is a bar with a nub from which a plumb line hangs. Two priests stood on a north–south line at night; one held the merkhet's plumb line, the other sighted through the bay so the line, the plumb and a star (e.g. a star crossing the meridian) coincided — the method for laying out temple axes and telling night hours by decan stars. The merkhet also doubled as a shadow clock (laid flat east–west in the morning, flipped in the afternoon). Clement of Alexandria's *Procession of Osiris* has the *horologos* (hour-priest) carrying "the astronomical palm" and the four astrological books of Hermes. Surviving examples: a bay and merkhet in Berlin (Neues Museum) inscribed for the astronomer-priest Hor, c. 600 BC; the merkhet's inscription calls him "who knows the movement of the sun and every star". Titles on monuments: "inspector of the astronomers, the one who knows the heliacal rising of Sirius"; "astronomers of the temple of Amun-Re, who know the secrets of Nut". [ESTABLISHED] [V-SECONDARY joe-antognini/song-of-urania transcript (episode 32); Berlin object details MEMORY] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkhet
- A gnomon/shadow method for north is what Dash's equinox experiment revives (A2); Egyptian texts describe "stretching the cord" (*pedj-shes*) foundation rites in which the king and Seshat drive stakes while sighting the Great Bear ("Meskhetiu") — attested at Dendera/Edfu, later than Giza. [MEMORY]

8. **The equinox alignment.** Dash's experiment (a rod, a string, a shadow on the autumn equinox reproduces the pyramid's exact counter-clockwise error) is a perfect on-screen demonstration a scientist can do in the museum car park — and a way for the mummy to explain that the ancients used the simplest possible tools *deliberately*, after the machines.
9. **The 2025 "underground city" as the film's fake-out.** Real: an unpermitted satellite-radar team claimed 648 m shafts under Khafre, an AI-made image went viral, and the Ministry called it fake news. In the film the ASI's own propaganda can weaponise exactly this — releasing "discoveries" to draw scientists into a trap — while the real anomaly the mummy wants is the modest, boring, peer-reviewed L-shaped feature two metres down in the Western Cemetery.
10. **The Serapeum as a machine-age vault misread as a bull cemetery.** 24 hard-stone boxes of 40–62 tonnes in a 350 m tunnel, only four inscribed, a 28-day installation record on a stela, a sarcophagus of the Persian conqueror Cambyses blocking the door. Mainstream logistics are fully documented (sand, rollers, winches) — which lets the mummy reveal that the *rite* copied an older function: sealed stone containers for what must never wake.
11. **The Labyrinth of Hawara as a red herring or the true archive.** Herodotus's 3,000 rooms, the priests' refusal to show the underground level, Petrie's 110-tonne quartzite chamber, an unpublished 2008 radar map and a flooded site. Everything about it is a promise nobody has cashed — ideal third-act destination.
12. **Grand Egyptian Museum opening night.** A real building whose walls are sighted on Khufu's and Menkaure's pyramids, with an 83-tonne Ramesses in the atrium and all 5,398 Tutankhamun objects together for the first time since 1922. The resurrection can happen in the conservation centre below the galleries; the robot army's first appearance on the alabaster façade under the pyramids' sightline.
13. **The Dixon cedar (3341–3094 BC) and the Surid legend.** A sealed-shaft relic older than the pyramid, plus a medieval tale of a king who built the pyramids to preserve knowledge before a world-ending flood — both real, both unexplained, both ready-made for the mummy's "we were warned" monologue.
