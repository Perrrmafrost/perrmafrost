# LOCATION & PROP BIBLE — LOCKED
## We're From The Future · S01E01 "The Package"

---

# PART ONE — THE HOUSE

**Asset ID:** `LOC-WARD-HOUSE` · single practical location, six spaces

## Design brief
A small 1950s single-storey rental on an ordinary street. **It must look like a
home, not a set, and specifically like a home somebody made an effort with.**
Lauren has made this place nice. That effort is the tragedy: she has been
decorating a hiding place.

The house's one strange quality is not visible as strangeness. It is an
*absence*, and the audience should feel it two acts before they can name it.

## THE ABSENCE — the house's hidden rule
**There is not a single photograph anywhere in this house.**

Not on the walls, not on the fridge, not in a frame on a shelf, not in a book.
Set dressing must be audited against this before every shot. Everything else is
here — postcards, a calendar, a takeaway menu, a child's drawing given by a
neighbour, ticket stubs — everything except a photograph of a person.

The one deliberate flag is the hallway: **four bare picture hooks in a row**,
with four faint unfaded rectangles behind them. It reads as "they haven't got
round to it." It is explained in Act Three in a single line inside an argument
and never mentioned again.

## Space by space

### `LOC-EXT` — Exterior / Cedar Row
Low houses, chain-link, mature trees, a basketball hoop with no net, wheelie bins
out too long. Two doors down there is a house that has parties. **That house must
be visible and lit in every exterior**; its ordinary happiness is the episode's
control group.

*Continuity:* dry and golden in the teaser. Light rain from 03:15 onward,
continuous, never heavy. Every house has its lights on in Act Four except theirs.

### `LOC-PORCH` — Front step
Concrete step, dark coir mat, aluminium screen door, a wall lamp with one dead
bulb, peeling white trim. **The mat is soaked in Act One and the box on it is
bone dry.** That contrast is a plot point; light it so the wet mat reads.

### `LOC-ENTRY` — Entry hall
Coat hook, shoes lined against the skirting, and the front door: solid, painted,
with a **frosted glass panel** and a **brass deadbolt.**

*The deadbolt is the second most important prop in the episode.* It is turned
twice in the teaser, checked with a palm, and turns itself in Act Four. Cast it
like a character: heavy, brass, worn bright at the thumbturn, and audible.

*The frosted panel* is the last image of the episode. Frame the entry so that the
panel is visible, deep, from the living room, in every wide from Act Three on.

### `LOC-HALL` — Hallway
Narrow, inoffensive wallpaper, a cheap hardware-store wall calendar, and the four
bare hooks. A closet at the end with coats, and above them, on the shelf, **two
grey canvas duffels with six years of dust on top.** They are visible, if you
look, from the teaser onward.

### `LOC-KITCHEN` — Kitchen
Small, warm, genuinely used. Mismatched wooden chairs, scratched laminate table,
open shelves, one good knife in a block, a bad pan, a notepad on the fridge under
a magnet. A **microwave with a digital clock** (it steps backward in Act Four).
A **smoke detector on the ceiling** with a dying battery.

*The overhead fixture* is the only hard light in the house and it is where they
put things they are afraid of. Both the box and the leaf get examined under it.

### `LOC-LIVING` — Living room
A worn fabric couch, one armchair, a low wooden coffee table, two table lamps, a
small television, a brick hearth with a **fire iron**, a curtained front window.

*The coffee table* becomes an altar. From Act Two the leaf is centred on it and
the room's blocking orbits that centre.

*The window* is where Miles checks the street. Establish his exact sightline in
the teaser (SC-05) and reuse the identical angle in Act Four (SC-20) so the
figure appears in a place the audience already knows he looks.

# PART TWO — THE PACKAGE

**Asset ID:** `PRP-BOX-01`

A small plain brown corrugated box, roughly 30 × 22 × 4 cm. Unbranded.

**What makes it wrong is entirely negative space:**
- No carrier logo, no service class, no barcode from any real system
- No handling wear: no crush, no scuff, no depot smear, no thumbprint
- Corners geometrically sharp
- **Completely dry, on a soaked mat, in the rain**
- Placed dead centre on the step and squared to its edge

> Squaring it to the step is the detail that does the most work. It says a person
> stood here, took care, and left. Use a set square. It will read.

## `PRP-LABEL-01` — the shipping label
Plain white, printed, and correct at a glance in every respect except the ones
that matter.

- Their address, correctly formatted
- **No sender. No return address. No carrier name.**
- A **barcode shape** — bars that look right and do not repeat the way barcodes
  repeat. A viewer who pauses must find it wrong; a viewer who doesn't must find
  it unremarkable.
- Handwritten in blue ballpoint, small and neat: `1 of 1`, and a six-digit
  consignment number
- **The seven in that number is crossed.**
- A consignment date: **14 October**

The crossed seven is shot twice: once as Lauren's aborted stroke in the teaser,
once here. **Match the pen weight and the angle of the crossbar between the two
inserts.** If a viewer compares frames, they should match. That is the payoff.

# PART THREE — THE LEAF

**Asset ID:** `PRP-LEAF-01` · the hero prop

## Physical specification
| | |
|---|---|
| Size | Approximately 240 × 320 mm — a large hardback |
| Thickness | 3 mm. Magazine-thin. |
| Weight | ~1.9 kg. Far heavier than it reads. |
| Finish | Matte, non-reflective, the colour of wet slate |
| Edges | Impossibly fine. Not chamfered, not radiused — *thin*. |
| Marks | **None.** No frame, no bezel, no port, no screw, no seam, no button, no logo, no serial, no regulatory text, on either face. |

**It must not read as a tablet.** The test: photograph it face-down. It should be
impossible to tell which side is the screen. If a crew member can tell, redesign it.

**Aesthetic direction:** not futuristic. *Finished.* It looks like the last
version of an object rather than an early one — the way a river stone looks
compared to a quarry stone. Expensive, ceremonial, and slightly too resolved to
have been manufactured by anyone we know.

## The four rules — consistent, never broken

| # | Rule | Established | Exploited |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No power.** It has no source and never runs out. | SC-09 | SC-12: the breaker is thrown, the house dies, the pane doesn't |
| 2 | **It renders; it does not reflect.** The room appears in its surface ~0.5s late. | SC-09 | Never explained. It is the answer to what the object is. |
| 3 | **Inverted temperature.** The face toward the room is cold; the face away is warm. | SC-09 | SC-12: a cold brass drawer pull, a warm cooler lid |
| 4 | **It responds to Lauren, not to Miles.** | SC-11 | SC-18: Lauren uses the rule deliberately to ask it a question |

## Containment behaviour — how failure is played
Every attempt to contain it fails **quietly**. No sparks, no shaking, no sound.

| Attempt | Failure |
|---|---|
| Face-down in a drawer | The brass drawer pull is cold |
| Sealed in a camping cooler | The cooler lid is warm |
| House power cut at the breaker | Everything dies; the pane glows |
| Covered with a jacket | The canvas lights up from underneath |

> The horror is arithmetic, never effects. A man puts his hand on a cooler lid
> and takes it away again. That is the whole scene.

## Display vocabulary — the complete list
The leaf shows exactly five things in this episode. Nothing else. Ever.

1. **An eye** — under one second, photographic, wet, blinking once
2. **A face** — the double, in darkness, lit from somewhere low and moving
3. **Four words** — `DO NOT GO BACK`, thin white wide-tracked type
4. **A number** — the countdown, small, beneath
5. **A photograph** — the motel, held four seconds

**The type on the leaf is the same typeface as the main title card.** This is
the episode's quietest and most disturbing joke: the show's own titles are in the
house style of whoever sent the box.

# PART FIVE — THE OTHER PLACES

### `LOC-MOTEL` — Blue Ridge Motel (still image + footage background)
Low two-storey, external walkway, twelve doors, sodium light, hard rain. A tall
yellow pole sign: **BLU RIDG MOT L** — the E and the second E dead.

Appears twice: badly out of focus behind the footage in SC-11, and as a held
still in SC-18. **The same sign, the same dead letters, the same angle of rain.**
The whole point is that Lauren recognises it across nine minutes of screen time.

Room eleven, second floor, end of the walkway, is the crossing. It is never
entered this season.

### `LOC-ELSEWHERE` — the footage
Unidentifiable. Night, hard rain, harsh unmotivated light, wet asphalt, a chain
fence. Under four seconds, degraded, narrower than the master aspect. **No face
resolves fully. Cut on movement.**

### `LOC-PALE` — the tag
A room lit uniformly from every direction so that nothing casts a shadow and no
corner can be located. Surfaces without visible joins. No furniture but a single
waist-height working surface. **Scale deliberately ambiguous** — it may be small
or enormous, and nothing in frame settles it.

Shoot it clean, bright and boring. The tag is frightening because it is
administrative.
