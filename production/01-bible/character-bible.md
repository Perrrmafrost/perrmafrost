## Body language
- **Default:** stillness. Miles does not fidget. When he is frightened, he becomes
  *more* still, not less. This is the single most useful direction on the page.
- **Hands:** goes quiet in the hands before he lies. Fingers flatten against a
  surface — table, thigh, doorframe.
- **Spatial habit:** places himself between Lauren and any door, window or object
  of interest. Unconsciously, constantly. Blocking should honour this in every
  scene without ever remarking on it.
- **Under stress:** speaks *slower*, not faster. Shortens sentences. Uses her
  name more.
- **Tell:** when he is about to withhold something, he answers the question
  before it is finished.

## Identity lock — the double
**Asset ID:** `CHR-LAUREN-02` ("the woman on the leaf") · **Look ID:** `CHR-LAUREN-02-L1`

Built from `CHR-LAUREN-01`. **Same age. Same face. Three deviations only:**

1. **A scar** — fine, pale, ~4 cm, running from below the left eye toward the
   ear along the cheekbone. Old. Well-healed. Lauren does not have it.
2. **Stillness.** Lauren's face is never at rest; the double's is. She does not
   perform listening. She does not blink on Lauren's rhythm. Reduce blink rate
   to roughly one-third.
3. **Hair down.** Loose, unstyled, longer. The only wardrobe signal: something
   dark, high-necked, matte, unreadable. No costume detail may be legible.

> **Direction:** She is not sinister. She is *tired,* and kind, and she has come
> a very long way to say something short. The uncanniness must come entirely
> from correctness — a face too exactly right, holding too exactly still.
>
> **The critical performance note:** she addresses Lauren, but **her eyes track
> Miles.** In every shot she appears in. Never remarked on. This is the episode's
> deepest rewatch payload.

## Body language (Lauren)
- **Default:** tactile. She touches doorframes, mugs, the back of Miles's neck,
  the box. She meets the world with her hands. **She touches the leaf before she
  reads the label** — that instinct is the character.
- **Under stress:** she gets *closer*, not further. She moves toward the thing.
- **When hurt:** she stops moving entirely and lowers her voice. This is her only
  resemblance to Miles and it should be visible.
- **Tell:** when she already knows the answer, she asks the question anyway, and
  watches his hands while he answers.

## Voice profile
| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Register | Mid, warm, slightly breathy on low volume |
| Pace | Quick, musical, interrupts easily, trails off when thinking |
| Volume ceiling | She *can* raise her voice — once, in Act 3 — and it must be the loudest human sound in the episode |
| Texture | Smile audible in the timbre even when the line isn't funny |
| Laugh | Genuine, unguarded, slightly loud — the sound of the life they built |
| Accent | Neutral North American, unforced. Unlike Miles she sounds native. She adapted better and that is a plot point. |
| Direction | "She is the only person in this house who is not performing. That is why she is the one it talks to." |

## Voice profile — the double
Same instrument, three changes: **slower, flatter, and no smile in the timbre.**
Consonants slightly over-precise. No breath sounds before phrases — she does not
appear to inhale before speaking. Do not pitch-shift, do not process, do not add
reverb or artefacts. **The horror is that it is clean.**

# THE RELATIONSHIP

## How they actually are together
- They finish each other's *logistics*, not each other's sentences.
- Their affection is physical and unremarked: a hand at the back of a neck, a
  foot hooked over an ankle, a mug handed over without being asked for.
- They have private comedy with no setup — a callback to a joke we never heard.
  **Write at least three of these and explain none of them.**
- They disagree the way long couples do: fast, low-stakes, resolved without
  ceremony. When the real fight comes in Act 3, its *unfamiliar rhythm* is the
  signal that something has broken.
- Neither says "I love you" in this episode. It is in every scene and in no line.

## The fracture
Miles's error is not that he lied. It is that **he decided which of them got to
be prepared.** Lauren's Act 3 line — *"You didn't protect me. You just made sure
I'd be the one who was surprised."* — is the thesis of their conflict and should
be the most quietly delivered line in the episode.

## Framing rule (see lookbook)
Shared frames in the teaser. Singles begin in Act Two. Through the fight they
share frames only on the room's widest lens, at opposite ends of it.

**The load-bearing rule is touch, not framing.** They make physical contact
exactly three times: the teaser's photo-refusal, the interrupted near-repair in
Act Three, and — twelve minutes later — the final shot. Nothing else in the
episode does as much work for as little. The build asserts the count.

# CASTING/GENERATION NOTES

- Both leads must read as **people, not models.** Preserve pores, asymmetry,
  flyaway hair, the natural flush in Miles's cheeks, the loose strands at
  Lauren's temples.
- **Negative, all character shots:** airbrushed skin, plastic sheen, symmetrical
  faces, whitened teeth, glamour lighting, styled hair, wet-look lips, fashion
  poses, over-sharpened eyes, catchlights that don't match the practicals.
- Faces must be legible in the dark. Shadow depth is a grade decision, never a
  reason to lose an eye.
