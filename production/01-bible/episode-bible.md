# WE'RE FROM THE FUTURE
## Episode Bible — Season 1, Episode 1: "The Package"

**Format:** Premium serialized science-fiction mystery drama
**Runtime target:** 32:00 (teaser + five acts + tag), excluding credits
**Aspect ratio:** 2.00:1 master · 24 fps
**Rating intent:** TV-14 — no gore, no on-screen violence, dread carried by performance

---

## 1. LOGLINE

A couple who have spent years hiding the fact that they are not from this century
receive an unmarked package containing a device that should not exist — and the
face on its screen is hers.

---

## 2. THE ONE-SENTENCE ENGINE

*The cost of an ordinary life is that somebody else paid for it.*

Every episode of this series returns to that. Miles and Lauren got a quiet house,
a Tuesday, a smoke detector that needs a battery — and the arithmetic of how they
got it is what the series slowly, cruelly performs.

---

## 3. WHAT THIS EPISODE IS ACTUALLY ABOUT

Not time travel. Time travel is the pressure, not the subject.

The subject is **a marriage in which one person has been quietly, lovingly
breaking the rule that keeps them both alive.** Miles has been sending a single
line forward — once a year, on the same date — to tell someone they are okay.
It is the most tender thing he does and it is the thing that gets them found.

Lauren's discovery is not "we've been located." It is:
*You didn't receive a package. You received a reply.*

That is the pilot. Everything else is architecture around it.

---

## 4. THE RULES OF THE WORLD

### 4.1 Where they are from
Not stated. Not this episode, not next season. The show holds this as a hard
line and — critically — **holds it in-world, not authorially.** Miles and Lauren
are forbidden to say the year out loud. That is a clause of their exile. The
audience is denied the date because the *characters* are denied it, which makes
the withholding feel like tension instead of coyness.

Inference the audience is allowed to make: near enough that the world is
recognizable, far enough that the difference is procedural rather than
aesthetic. Their future is not chrome. It is *administrative.*

### 4.2 The Terms
The conditions of their stay. Both leads know them by heart. They are never
recited in full in one piece — they surface in fragments, the way a couple
quotes a lease or a prayer.

> **ONE.** Do not be found.
> **TWO.** Do not be recorded.
> **THREE.** Do not go back.
> **FOUR.** Do not answer.
> **FIVE.** Do not send anything forward.

Design notes:
- Clause Two is why there are **no photographs in the house.** Bare picture
  hooks in the hallway. The audience notices in the cold open, understands in
  Act 3.
- Clause Five is **what Miles broke.**
- Clause Three is what the device's Act 3 message invokes — and the reason
  Miles goes white is not the message's content, it is its *phrasing.*
  Whoever sent it has the Terms. Which means it came from inside.
- Clause Four is what they spend Acts 4–5 failing to obey.

### 4.3 Vocabulary discipline
The entire series operates on five in-world words. No more. Coined jargon is
how sci-fi dies.

| Word | Meaning | First use |
|---|---|---|
| **the Terms** | The conditions of their exile | Act 2 |
| **the office** | Whoever administers the Terms. Said dismissively. | Act 3 |
| **carrying** | Crossing between times | Act 5 |
| **the leaf** | The device (Miles's word — the fact he *has* a word is the tell) | Act 1 |
| **the crossing** | The place/moment they arrived | Act 5 |

Never used: timeline, paradox, temporal, portal, quantum, anomaly (except by the
automated system voice in the tag, where its bureaucratic flatness is the joke).

### 4.4 How the device behaves — consistent, rule-based
The leaf is not magic. It obeys four rules, established early and never broken:

1. **It does not need power.** It has none and never runs out.
2. **It renders, it does not reflect.** The room appears in its surface roughly
   half a second late. This is the single most important physical detail in the
   episode — it tells the attentive viewer the leaf is a *window*, not a screen,
   long before anyone says so.
3. **Its temperature is inverted.** The face toward the room is cold. The face
   toward the wall is warm.
4. **It responds to Lauren and not to Miles.** Proximity, touch, voice. Miles can
   carry it, cover it, box it — nothing. Lauren walks past it and it wakes.

Containment attempts fail *quietly.* No sparks, no shaking. Miles puts it in a
cooler; the cooler is warm. He puts it face-down in a drawer; the drawer's brass
pull is cold. The horror is in the arithmetic, never the effects budget.

## 5. THE KNOWLEDGE LEDGER

The single most important document in the writers' room. Three columns, held
strictly.

### 5.1 What the AUDIENCE knows by end of Episode 1
- Miles and Lauren are not from this time.
- Their stay is conditional, governed by five rules.
- Miles broke rule five — once a year, one line, to tell someone they're alive.
- The package is dated to that same annual date. It is a reply.
- The woman on the leaf has Lauren's face and voice, a scar Lauren doesn't have,
  and a stillness Lauren doesn't have.
- She knows a phrase from Lauren's childhood that Lauren has never said aloud
  in this century.
- She claims she is the version who obeyed — who left when told.
- Something has crossed over and is at the door.
- The voice at the door is one Miles knows.

### 5.2 What the CHARACTERS know that the audience does not
| Fact | Held by | Released |
|---|---|---|
| Their real names | Both | S1 mid-season |
| The year they left | Both | Deliberately never |
| Who Miles has been writing to | Miles | E2 cold open |
| What happened the night of the crossing | Miles (fully), Lauren (partly) | E3 |
| Why Lauren was told to stop crossing her sevens | Lauren | E4 |
| Whether Lauren agreed to the Terms or was signed into them | Lauren (suspects) | S1 finale |

### 5.3 What NOBODY knows yet — the series' load-bearing mysteries
1. Who sent the leaf.
2. Whether the woman on the screen is a copy, a recording, a branch, or Lauren.
3. What the countdown was counting to (the door is the *answer*, not the cause).
4. Why Lauren's file was marked LOCATED years before Miles's.
5. What "nothing crosses over whole" costs the thing at the door.

**Rule of release:** one load-bearing mystery may be *narrowed* per episode.
None may be closed before its season. A mystery that is answered must
immediately be replaced by a better one in the same scene.

## 8. THE COUNTDOWN — the pilot's spine

At the end of Act Three the leaf displays a duration and begins counting down.
**It runs in real time with the episode.** It is not decorative; the audience can
track it, and it reaches 00:00 at the exact moment of the knock in Act Five.

- Appears: 19:10 (episode runtime) reading **12:00**
- Visible again: 22:40 (**08:30**), 26:15 (**04:55**), 29:50 (**01:20**)
- Reaches 00:00: 31:10 — the knock

The characters never learn what it counts to. The audience finds out with them,
at the door. This is the difference between a mystery and a stall: the pilot's
central unanswered question is answered *by an image*, in the last thirty seconds,
and the answer opens something worse.

## 9. TONE CONTRACT — the seven prohibitions

Binding on script, board, shot prompt, edit and grade.

1. **No exposition monologue.** Backstory arrives only as a weapon inside an
   argument. If a line explains something and costs the speaker nothing, cut it.
2. **No holograms, no floating UI, no neon.** The future is a flat pane and a
   piece of paper tape.
3. **No jump scares.** Two loud moments in the whole episode, both diegetic
   (the doorbell; the breaker).
4. **No score under revelation.** The three biggest turns play dry. Music is for
   approach and aftermath, never for the blow itself.
5. **No character says a thing the other already knows** for the audience's sake.
6. **No running, no screaming, no dropped keys.** They are competent adults who
   have rehearsed being found for years. Their competence *is* the horror.
7. **No answer without a worse question** in the same scene.

## 10. EMOTIONAL ARC MAP

### Miles
| Segment | State | Governing behaviour | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teaser | Ease, maintained | Performs relaxation. Checks locks anyway. | Open, loose, watches windows |
| Act 1 | Recognition suppressed | Handles the box too carefully | Stills. Hands go quiet. |
| Act 2 | Management | Tries to make it a malfunction | Between Lauren and the object |
| Act 3 | Exposure | Confesses the smaller truth to hide the larger | Can't sit. Won't face her. |
| Act 4 | Protective panic | Plans, packs, counts exits | Fast, clipped, competent |
| Act 5 | Surrender | Stops lying because there's no time left | Still. Finally still. |

**Miles's lie is not cowardice. It is tenderness.** He broke the rule to tell
someone they were alive. Play every beat of his concealment as love that has
gone bad in storage.

### Lauren
| Segment | State | Governing behaviour | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teaser | At home, genuinely | Fills the house. Wants the hooks filled. | Relaxed, tactile, warm |
| Act 1 | Unease before understanding | Touches the box before she reads it | Drawn toward it |
| Act 2 | Recognition | Refuses to let Miles close the subject | Forward, insistent |
| Act 3 | Fury, earned | Names the real injury: unpreparedness | Stops moving. Very quiet. |
| Act 4 | Grief | The phrase from childhood undoes her | Sits down. Hands over mouth. |
| Act 5 | Resolve | Decides they answer the door | Stands. Puts herself in front. |

**Lauren's anger is not "you lied."** It is *"you decided which of us got to be
ready."* That distinction is the whole marriage.

### The relationship — the shape of the hour
```
warmth ──── unease ──── friction ──── rupture ──── near-repair ──── alliance
teaser      act one     act two      act three     act four        act five
                                         ↑              ↑
                              the withheld truth   they almost make it back
                                                   the leaf interrupts
```
They must be closest, physically, in the final shot — and it must have cost them
the entire episode to get there.

## 11. THE LAST THIRTY SECONDS

Locked, because everything is built toward it.

- The leaf speaks its last line to Lauren.
- A knock: **three, then two.** A pattern. Miles knows the pattern.
- From outside, muffled, *warm*: **"It's me."**
- Miles's face — because that is the answer to who he has been writing to.
- Cut to black before the door opens.

"It's me" instead of "open the door," because intimacy is more frightening than
threat, and because it retroactively converts Miles's secret from a mistake into
a summons.

---

## 12. THE AUDIENCE'S EXIT FEELINGS — the QC target

The episode has failed if a first-time viewer cannot produce all six:

1. These two people feel like they have actually lived together.
2. Something is deeply wrong and I can't point at an effect that told me so.
3. I need to know what Miles was sending.
4. Why does that thing have her face — and why is it watching *him*?
5. What was the countdown counting to, if the door was the answer?
6. Who says "it's me" like that?
