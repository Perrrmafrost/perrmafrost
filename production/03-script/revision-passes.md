# PHASE 3 — REVISION PASSES
## Five passes over the draft, with what each one changed

Each pass was run against the whole script with one question only. Findings are
recorded whether or not they resulted in a change, because "considered and kept"
is a decision too.

---

## PASS 1 — DIALOGUE NATURALNESS

**Question:** Can an actor say this line without adjusting it?

| # | Was | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | "You don't understand what that device is capable of." | *"I've held one."* | Nobody has ever said "capable of" in a kitchen. Three words carry the same information and a confession. |
| 1.2 | "We made a promise to never speak of our past." | *"We agreed we wouldn't go looking!"* | Promises are not described, they are invoked mid-argument. |
| 1.3 | Miles explains the smoke detector is annoying. | *"There it is." / "I'll get a battery." / "You've said that." / "And I've meant it every time."* | Four lines, no information, complete marriage. |
| 1.4 | Lauren: "This is impossible!" | *"There's a barcode." / "There's a barcode* shape*."* | Characters in this show do not announce impossibility. They correct each other's nouns. |
| 1.5 | Miles: "I'm scared too, Lauren." | *"I have been so scared for so long that I forgot it was a thing that could end."* | The only line in the episode where Miles is allowed to be articulate. He has earned it, and it costs him. |

**Kept despite flagging:** the double's dialogue is *deliberately* unnatural — full
sentences, no contractions in three places, no self-interruption. This is her
only characterization device and must survive every naturalness note.

## PASS 2 — EXPOSITION REDUCTION

**Rule applied:** if a line explains something and costs the speaker nothing, cut it.

**Deleted entirely:**
- A speech in Act 2 where Miles explains that they travelled from the future. *(The audience assembles this from "don't go back," "we came out," and "six years." They are faster than the script.)*
- A speech in Act 3 where Miles explains who administers the Terms. *(Replaced with the single word "they," used eleven times and never defined.)*
- Lauren asking "so what year are you from?" *(Fatal. Cut on sight. The characters are forbidden to say it — see bible §4.1.)*
- Any use of: timeline, paradox, temporal, portal, quantum, anomaly. *(Zero instances survive in dialogue. "Anomaly" appears nowhere; the tag's system voice was rewritten to avoid it.)*

**Converted from statement to weapon:**

| Fact | Delivery mechanism |
|---|---|
| They are not from this time | An argument about whether the rule was broken |
| The Terms exist | Lauren reciting them to win a point |
| Six years have passed | Lauren correcting him: *"Six years and four months"* |
| Miles has broken clause five | Extracted from him one syllable at a time under questioning |
| They arrived at a motel | A location he identifies too precisely: *"Room eleven. Second floor, end of the walkway."* |
| Crossing is fatal | A warning given with 41 seconds left |

**Net exposition budget:** the total quantity of backstory in the episode is
delivered in 41 lines, of which 38 occur inside an active conflict.

## PASS 3 — CHARACTER CONSISTENCY

**Miles.** Checked every line against the bible's tell — *he answers before the
question finishes* — and against the direction that he gets *quieter* under
pressure.

- Found: Miles interrupted Lauren four times. Three are correct (concealment).
  One, in Act 5, was just pace. **Cut** — he now lets her finish and answers late,
  which is worse.
- Found: Miles raised his voice in the Act 3 fight. **Removed.** Lauren has the
  only shout in the episode. Miles's loudest line is *"It's nine words,"* and it
  is spoken almost inaudibly. The asymmetry is the characterization.
- Verified: Miles physically places himself between Lauren and a door/window/object
  in **nine** scenes. Never remarked on. Logged to the continuity matrix as a
  blocking requirement, not an actor's choice.

**Lauren.** Checked against *she moves toward the thing* and *she touches first.*

- Verified: she touches the box before reading it; she puts her hand on the pane
  to stop Miles; she crouches to the leaf's level to speak to it; she sits on the
  floor when the phrase lands. She never once backs away from the object. Her
  only retreat in the episode is from *Miles*, in Act 3.
- Added: her hand closing on the pendant in Act 4 — the bible's "reflex older
  than the marriage." It was in the character document and missing from the page.

**The double.** Verified she never answers Miles. Verified her eyeline is on Miles
in every appearance and breaks *once*, on the final line. Verified she takes no
audible breath before speaking. Verified she hesitates exactly once — before
"Because I—" — which is the only moment she is deciding rather than delivering.

## PASS 4 — PACING

Measured in scene lengths and silence placement.

- **Act 2 was long.** The argument ran 90 seconds before the leaf woke. Trimmed to
  70; the leaf now interrupts Miles mid-word (*"This is—"*), which is both faster
  and funnier and more frightening.
- **Act 3 was flabby in the middle.** Lauren's walk to the calendar was covered in
  dialogue. **Now silent** — she reads the label, walks out, stands, comes back.
  Fourteen seconds of a woman doing arithmetic. It is the best passage in the act.
- **Act 4 had five impossibilities.** Cut to four. The fifth (a song distorting on
  a speaker) was the weakest and its removal lets the smoke-detector chirp land as
  the act's centrepiece.
- **Silence audit.** Nine deliberate silences of 3+ seconds, distributed: teaser 1,
  A1 2, A2 2, A3 2, A4 1, A5 1. None fall within 90 seconds of another. The
  longest (Act 5, after "Did it come from me?") runs three seconds of a man not
  answering, and is the most important pause in the episode.
- **Act-out audit.** All five act breaks land on an image, not a line: an eye; a
  sign nobody will admit seeing; a number starting to count; a deadbolt turning
  itself; a shape behind frosted glass.

## PASS 5 — MYSTERY PRESERVATION

Audited against the bible's knowledge ledger. Every load-bearing mystery must
survive the episode.

| Mystery | Status after final draft | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| What year they are from | **Intact.** Never stated, never implied numerically. | Protected in-world by the Terms |
| Who sent the leaf | **Intact.** | "Somebody over there" is as far as it goes |
| What the double is | **Narrowed, not answered.** | She claims to be the version who complied. She may be lying. Nothing corroborates her. |
| Who Miles writes to | **Intact — and now sharpened.** | Answered visually in the last five seconds by a voice, not by a name |
| Why Lauren's voice | **Intact.** | Deliberately never addressed. Lauren asks; the transmission breaks on the answer. |
| What the countdown counts to | **Answered by an image, in the last 30 seconds.** | This is the pilot's one permitted closure — and the answer opens two worse questions |
| What Lauren doesn't remember | **Newly opened.** | *"I don't remember any of that."* / *"I know."* Replaces a spent mystery with a better one, per tone contract §7 |
| Lauren's real name | **Opened and immediately withheld.** | The audio hole in Act 4 |
| Why Lauren's file predates Miles's | **Opened in the tag.** | Nineteen years. Unexplained. |

**Contract check — §7.7, "no answer without a worse question in the same scene":**

- Act 3 answers *why now* (Miles has been signalling) → opens *who is he sorry to.*
- Act 4 answers *where to go* (the motel) → opens *why doesn't Lauren remember it.*
- Act 5 answers *who is at the door* (someone Miles knows) → opens *what did it cost them to get here.*
- Tag answers *are they being hunted* (yes) → opens *why was she found nineteen years ago.*

Pass.
