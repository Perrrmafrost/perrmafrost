# COLOUR GRADE GUIDE
## We're From The Future · S01E01 "The Package"

**Working space:** ACEScct · **Delivery:** Rec.709 100-nit and HDR10 1000-nit ·
**LUT:** one show LUT, film-emulation base, applied to every shot before any
secondary work

---

## THE GRADE IN ONE SENTENCE

> **The episode drains.** It begins as the warmest thing you have seen all week
> and ends nearly monochrome, and no viewer should be able to point at where it
> happened.

---

## THE ARC — measured, not felt

| Segment | Saturation | Colour temp | Black level | Contrast |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Teaser | **100%** (reference) | Warm, 3400K feel | Lifted, warm toe | Gentle |
| Act One | 94% | Warm, slightly cooler ambient | Lifted | Gentle |
| Act Two | 86% | Split: warm key / neutral pane | Slightly deeper | Medium |
| Act Three | 76% | Cooling | Deeper, still open | Medium |
| Act Four | 64% | Cold-dominant | Deep | Higher |
| Act Five | **52%** | Near-neutral, drained | Deepest | High, but faces protected |
| Final shot | 48% | Almost monochrome | Deepest in the episode | Faces still fully legible |
| Tag | **35%** | Perfectly neutral | **No blacks at all** | Flat |

**Rate of change:** roughly 8–10% saturation per act, applied as a linear ramp
*within* each act rather than as a step at the act break. Steps are visible;
ramps are not.

## THE THREE PALETTES

### WARM (household)
- Key light 3200K, gently desaturated in the highlights so it reads as tungsten,
  not as orange
- **Skin sits slightly warm and slightly rosy.** Preserve Miles's natural flush
  across the cheeks and nose — do not neutralise it, it is a character trait
- Shadows are warm-neutral, never blue
- **Reds are the only vivid colour permitted in the episode, and only here**

### COLD (the pane)
- 6000–6500K, **very low saturation.** The critical note: it is not *blue*, it is
  *drained*. Push it toward blue and the show becomes generic instantly
- Almost no falloff — the light does not behave like a lamp, and the grade should
  not help it
- A small amount of halation on white type only

### PALE (the tag)
- Perfectly neutral, no cast at all
- **Black point raised until there is no black in the frame**
- Contrast flattened. Nothing models. Nothing has a shadow
- The most unsettling image in the episode is graded like a product catalogue

---

## HARD RULES

1. **Faces are never sacrificed to blacks.** In every night interior, the darkest
   part of a face sits at or above IRE 8. If a performance is unreadable, the
   shot is regraded, not accepted. Depth of black is a taste; losing an eye is a
   failure.
2. **No teal in the shadows. Ever.** The single fastest way to make this show
   look like everything else.
3. **No day-for-night blue wash.** Night is dark and warm-lit from lamps until
   the blackout, and then it is dark and lit by one cold source.
4. **No vignette.** The house is not judging anyone.
5. **No power windows on faces** except to *recover* an eye, never to shape one.
6. **Grain is uniform and present in every frame**, including the black frames at
   act-outs. A genuinely clean black frame breaks the material.

## SHOT-SPECIFIC NOTES

| Shot | Note |
|---|---|
| Opening wide (SH0001) | The warmest, most saturated frame in the episode. It is the reference the whole grade descends from. Grade it first. |
| The label inserts | Match the two crossed-seven inserts (teaser / Act One) **exactly** — same white point, same blue in the ballpoint ink. A viewer comparing frames must find them identical. |
| The eye (SH0090-ish) | Photographic iris. No enhancement, no added catchlight, no sharpening. It should look like a frame of documentary that got in by mistake. |
| The double, all appearances | **Her skin must match Lauren's exactly** — same tone, same saturation, same warmth. The only permitted difference is the scar. If the grade makes her cooler or paler, it explains her, and she must not be explained. |
| The footage (SH0138-ish) | Genuinely degraded: crushed, blown, chroma-subsampled, narrower gamut. It should look like a different acquisition format because it is. |
| The blackout (SC-20) | The street outside stays fully warm and saturated. **The contrast between their dead house and the living street is the shot.** Do not balance them. |
| The final wide | The lowest saturation in the episode. Both faces still legible, lit from one cold source below. The frosted panel holds a soft, slightly warmer rectangle of streetlight — the only warmth left in the frame, and it is behind them. |

## HDR

The HDR pass exists for one shot: **the pane in a dark house.**

- SDR peak: 100 nits. HDR peak: **220 nits, on the pane only.**
- Nothing else in the episode goes above 180.
- The specular range is spent on a piece of glass in a living room, which is the
  correct place to spend it.
- **No HDR treatment of the tag.** It is flat in both deliverables.

---

## QC GATES

The grade is rejected if any of the following are true:

- [ ] Any face in any shot has an unreadable eye
- [ ] Any shadow reads as teal or blue-green
- [ ] The double's skin does not match Lauren's within 2% saturation
- [ ] The two crossed-seven inserts do not match
- [ ] Saturation does not descend monotonically across the six segments
- [ ] Any frame is free of grain
- [ ] The street in SC-20 is not visibly warmer and more saturated than the house
