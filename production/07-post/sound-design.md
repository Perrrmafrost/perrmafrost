# SOUND DESIGN PLAN
## We're From The Future · S01E01 "The Package"

**Mix format:** 5.1 theatrical fold-down to stereo · dialogue-led · -24 LKFS

---

## 1. THE PRINCIPLE

> **This episode is scary because of what you can hear in a quiet house, and
> terrifying twice, because the house stops.**

There are two loud sounds in thirty-six minutes: the doorbell and the breaker.
Everything else is built from a domestic sound bed and its removal. **Silence is
the effects budget.**

---

## 2. ROOM TONES — recorded, not synthesised

Every space gets its own tone, recorded at length and laid continuously. Cuts
between spaces are *audible* as changes in the floor, which is how a real house
sounds and how the audience learns the geography without a single establishing shot.

| ID | Space | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `RT-KIT` | Kitchen | Refrigerator compressor (cycles on/off — **cue the cycles deliberately**), extractor hood rattle, distant boiler |
| `RT-LIV` | Living room | Lower, wider. Wall clock, television at the edge of audibility, window resonance |
| `RT-HALL` | Hallway | Tightest, driest. Almost no reverb. |
| `RT-ENTRY` | Entry | Draught at the door. The outside is faintly present here and nowhere else. |
| `RT-EXT` | Street | Rain, a main road four streets away, the party two doors down, a dog |
| `RT-PALE` | The tag | **Acoustically dead.** No reflections at all. Deeply wrong to the ear and unnameable to the audience. |

**Rain** begins at 03:15 and runs continuously to the end. It never gets heavier.
It is the episode's metronome.

---

## 3. THE SIGNATURE SOUNDS

### `SFX-CHIRP` — the smoke detector
**The most important sound in the episode.** One recording, used eleven times,
never varied, never pitched, never re-recorded.

| Where | Function |
|---|---|
| Teaser | A domestic annoyance. Establishes the exact tone. |
| Act One, mid-unboxing | Makes them both flinch, then laugh. Discharges the tension. |
| End of Act Two | The last sound before the act-out silence. |
| **Act Four (SC-17)** | The detector chirps. **Then the living room chirps back — same tone, half a beat late.** |

That half-beat is the same half-beat as the reflection lag. Nobody says so.

**Mix note:** the answering chirp is **spatially placed at the coffee table**,
not centred. In 5.1 it is in the right surround. The audience locates it before
Miles does.

### `SFX-BOLT` — the deadbolt
Heavy brass, close-mic'd, unhurried. Turned twice in the teaser so the audience
learns it. In SC-20 it turns **one full revolution with nobody near it**, in a
house with no power and no room tone.

Cut to black **on** the sound, not after it.

### `SFX-LEAF` — the device signature
Restrained to the point of absence. The leaf has **no boot sound, no chime, no
hum, no power-up.** It wakes in silence.

Its only audio signature is a **filtered noise pulse arriving on each second of
the countdown** — around 40 Hz, ‑40 dB, felt in the chest and not identifiable as
a sound. It enters when the countdown starts and continues, unbroken, to zero.

> If a listener can consciously hear it, it is 6 dB too loud.

### `SFX-DOOR` — the knock
Five knuckle knocks on painted wood. Three, pause, two. **Dry, close, no reverb
tail** — recorded from the inside of the door, which is where we are.

It must not sound urgent. It sounds like someone who is expected.

---

## 4. THE AUTHORED SILENCES

Nine deliberate silences, each doing a different job. They are *composed*, not
left over.

| # | Runtime | What drops | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Teaser, after "We're not from around here" | Score only | A joke lands wrong |
| 2 | Act One, before opening the box | Score, then rain fades up | The decision |
| 3 | Act One, the cutting of the tape | Everything but the tape and the fridge | Foreground the small act |
| 4 | Act Two, before the leaf wakes | Score. Television recedes. | Make the audience relax |
| 5 | Act Two act-out | **Everything, on the frame** | The hardest cut in the episode |
| 6 | Act Three, Miles fails to say a name | Score | The name is never spoken |
| 7 | Act Three, after Lauren's shout | Everything, two seconds | The loudest moment makes the deepest hole |
| 8 | **Act Four, the redacted name** | **The entire bed, 0.5s** | See below |
| 9 | Act Five, after "Did it come from me?" | Everything, three seconds | The answer |

### The redacted name — SC-19
When the double says Lauren's real name, **the audio does not bleep it, mute it,
distort it or cover it with a sound effect. The entire sound bed is removed for
half a second** — room tone, rain, breath, all of it — and then resumes exactly
where it left off.

It is not censorship. It is a **hole in the recording**, and it must feel like
one. The absence is authored: the audience should experience a half-second in
which the world stops having sound, and be unable to say what happened.

### The last silence — SC-22
At 00:00 the countdown pulse stops and **the mix floor goes to absolute zero**
for eight seconds. No rain, no room tone, nothing. The audience must conclude the
episode has ended safely.

Then the knock.

## 5. SPATIAL LOGIC

The mix obeys the house, always. **The leaf is a point source**, and everything
it does comes from wherever it physically is:

| Act | Leaf position | Mix position |
|---|---|---|
| One | Kitchen table | Centre-front, close |
| Two–Four | Living room coffee table | Centre-front; when the camera is in the kitchen, **right surround** |
| Three | Inside a drawer / a cooler | Muffled, filtered, still correctly placed |
| Five | Inside Lauren's shoulder bag | Follows her. Moves with the actor. |

The party two doors down is **always in the rear field** and always slightly
warm. In Act Four, when their power dies and the street's does not, the party
comes *up* in the mix. The world getting louder while their house goes dark is
the single most effective thing in the whole soundtrack.

---

## 6. DIALOGUE

- Production sound is the intended sound. **ADR only where physically necessary.**
- **No noise reduction on the leads.** Breath, lip noise, clothing rustle and
  room are all part of the performance. A too-clean voice is the enemy.
- Lauren's one shout is the only line that peaks. It sits ~9 dB above the
  episode's dialogue average and is followed by two seconds of nothing.
- Miles's quietest lines (the nine words; "nothing comes through whole") are
  mixed **at the very bottom of intelligibility.** The audience should lean in.

### The double's voice
| Rule | |
|---|---|
| Processing | **None.** No pitch shift, no formant work, no reverb, no artefacts, no vocoder, no bit-crush. |
| Placement | Dead centre, dry, at exactly the same level as Lauren's own dialogue |
| Breath | **Removed.** No inhale before any phrase. This is the entire effect. |
| Timing | Slower. She never overlaps and never rushes. She does not react to interruption. |

> Every instinct will be to process her. Resist all of them. She is frightening
> because she is clean, close, and perfectly ordinary — and does not breathe.

### The voice at the door
Muffled through wood and a wall — a genuine acoustic treatment, not an EQ preset.
Warm, unhurried, **gender deliberately unclear.** Two syllables. It should sound
like an embrace.

## 7. SPOT EFFECTS — the working list

**Teaser:** lawnmower · sparrows · car pass · screen door · pans on a hob ·
mail on laminate · pen on paper · fridge door seal · television dialogue (low) ·
party bass · deadbolt ×2 · doorbell

**Act One:** rain on window · knife from block · **tape parting (close, almost
too loud)** · cardboard flaps · foam · the pane set on wood · a chair leg ·
refrigerator cycling on

**Act Two:** couch springs · a cushion · footsteps on board then carpet · a palm
on the pane (a dead sound — no resonance at all) · television credits, thin and
far away

**Act Three:** drawer runners · a cooler lid clipping · **the breaker** ·
the whole house stopping · a jacket dragged off a table

**Act Four:** phone buzz on laminate · a drawer closing on it · duffel zips ·
dust · the microwave compressor stuttering on the repeated minute · the chirp,
and its answer

**Act Five:** chair legs on carpet then board · a fire iron on wood · fabric ·
two people breathing · **the knock** · a voice through a door

**Tag:** an acoustically dead room · a finger on a surface · **no confirmation
sound when the status changes** — nothing acknowledges it, and that is the joke ·
a system voice with no reverb in a room with no reverb

---

## 8. THE MIX IN ONE LINE

Start with a house full of small sounds. Take one away each act. End with a knock
in a room that has none left.
